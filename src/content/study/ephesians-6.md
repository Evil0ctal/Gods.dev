The chapter starts in the living room — children, parents, servants, masters — and ends on a battlefield. The pivot word is "finally" (6:10), and it's worth noticing how much ordinary household instruction comes before Paul ever mentions armor.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible ephesians 6`

## Instructions that cut both ways

Verses 1-9 aren't one-directional. Children obey, but fathers are told not to provoke them to wrath (6:4). Servants obey, but masters are told to drop the threats, because their Master and the servants' Master is the same, and shows no partiality (6:9). Authority, in this passage, comes with its own restraints.

## The real opponent

> *"For our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against the principalities, against the powers, against the world's rulers of the darkness of this age, and against the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places."* — Ephesians 6:12

Paul has just spent a chapter and a half on family and workplace friction, then names something underneath all of it. Whatever conflict shows up at the dinner table, he says, isn't ultimately about the other person.

## Armor built to stand, not attack

Notice the repeated verb across 6:11-14: stand, withstand, stand, stand. Every piece of armor Paul lists — truth, righteousness, peace, faith, salvation, the word — is defensive gear for holding ground, not a weapon for gaining it. The posture of the whole passage is endurance, not conquest.

## Questions for study

1. Where do household instructions in verses 1-9 challenge whoever holds authority in that relationship, not just whoever's under it?
2. What "flesh and blood" conflict in your life might actually be about something underneath it, per 6:12?
3. Which piece of armor in 6:14-17 do you most need to "put on" right now?
