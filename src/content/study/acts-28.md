A viper bites Paul on Malta and the islanders wait for him to swell up
and drop dead, then flip completely and decide he's a god when he
doesn't (28:3-6). It's a small, almost comic episode, but it sets the
tone for the whole chapter: things happen to Paul, and Paul just keeps
doing what he was already doing — in this case, gathering sticks for a
fire.

## Hospitality before the finish line

Before Rome, there's Malta — three months of unexpected welcome from
people the text calls "barbarians" but describes acting with unusual
kindness (28:2). Paul heals the local official's father and then "the
rest also who had diseases in the island came and were cured" (28:9).
Even in a shipwreck's aftermath, on a detour nobody planned, there's
ministry to do and people willing to receive it.

## Rome, at last — and it's anticlimactic

After three books' worth of buildup — Paul's stated ambition to preach in
Rome, the Spirit's warnings, the appeals, the shipwreck — he finally
arrives, and Luke gives it almost no fanfare. Paul is allowed to live by
himself with a soldier guarding him (28:16), and his first move is to
call the local Jewish leaders together to explain himself, again, one
more time (28:17-20).

## The book that doesn't end

Acts closes with Paul "welcoming all who were coming to him, preaching
the Kingdom of God, and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ with all
boldness, without hindrance" (28:30-31) — two full years of it. There is
no account of his death, no verdict from Caesar, no tidy resolution. Luke
built an entire book toward Rome and then stops the moment the gospel is
simply, freely being preached there.

> *"Preaching the Kingdom of God, and teaching the things concerning the
> Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness, without hindrance."* — Acts 28:31

That word "unhindered" is the last word of the book. Acts doesn't end
because the story is over. It ends because the point was never Paul's
biography — it was the word's unstoppable progress, and that progress
doesn't need a bow tied on it.

## Questions for study

1. Why might Luke choose to end the book without telling us what happens
   to Paul?
2. What do you make of the islanders' whiplash from "he's a murderer" to
   "he's a god" — what does it say about judging by circumstance?
3. "Unhindered" is the book's last word. Where do you need to trust that
   the message can outlast its messenger?
4. Looking back across Acts as a whole, what has actually driven the
   story forward — human plans, or something else?
