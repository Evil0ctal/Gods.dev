Paul has already made his appeal for the Jerusalem collection in chapter
8. Chapter 9 is the follow-through — and it opens with him admitting he's
been bragging about the Corinthians to the Macedonians, using their
earlier enthusiasm to stir up more (9:2-4). It's an oddly candid piece of
fundraising strategy: he's not hiding the social pressure, he's naming
it, then immediately undercutting it with something better.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 2corinthians 9`

## Sowing decides the harvest

*"He who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly. He who sows
bountifully will also reap bountifully."* — 2 Corinthians 9:6

The farming image does real work here. A farmer doesn't sow to feel
generous in the moment; he sows because the harvest is determined by the
sowing. Paul isn't promising a financial return on giving — he's
describing a law of proportion that runs deeper than money: what you
release shapes what comes back to you, in ways you can't always predict
or control.

## Not grudgingly

Verse 7 is the chapter's center: giving "not grudgingly, or under
compulsion; for God loves a cheerful giver." The Greek behind "cheerful"
is where we get "hilarious" — giving with a kind of reckless, unforced
delight, the opposite of a number extracted through guilt.

## Grace multiplies grace

The chapter's closing move (9:10-15) reframes the whole transaction:
God, who supplies seed to the sower, will supply and multiply what the
Corinthians give, so that generosity increases rather than depletes the
giver. It ends on thanksgiving — "thanks be to God for his unspeakable
gift" (9:15) — because ultimately every gift given is downstream of one
already received.

## Questions for study

1. Where have you given "grudgingly" rather than cheerfully — and what
   was actually driving the reluctance?
2. Does Paul's sowing/reaping logic sound like a formula to you, or a
   description? What's the difference?
3. What "unspeakable gift" underwrites your own capacity to give
   anything at all?
