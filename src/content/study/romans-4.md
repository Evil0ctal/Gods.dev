Paul needs a witness his Jewish readers can't dismiss, so he calls the
one figure everyone already agrees is righteous: Abraham. And then he
makes a move that would have startled his first audience — he points out
that Abraham was declared righteous *before* he was circumcised (4:10).
The order matters. Circumcision came after, "a seal of the righteousness
of the faith which he had while he was uncircumcised" (4:11).

> Read it in the terminal: `bible romans 4`

## Credited, not earned

The chapter's key phrase, repeated from Genesis, is that Abraham
"believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness" (4:3).
Paul draws out the accounting language deliberately: to the one who
works, wages are owed, not gifted. But "to him who doesn't work, but
believes... his faith is accounted for righteousness" (4:4-5). It's the
language of a ledger where the debt gets marked paid without the
corresponding entry of labor.

> *"Now to him who works, the reward is not counted as grace, but as
> something owed. But to him who doesn't work, but believes in him who
> justifies the ungodly, his faith is accounted for righteousness."*
> — Romans 4:4-5

## A father before he had anything to show for it

The chapter's climax describes Abraham believing God's promise of a son
"against hope" (4:18), fully aware of his own body's deadness and
Sarah's, and yet "he didn't waver through unbelief" but "grew strong
through faith, giving glory to God" (4:20). This isn't naive optimism;
it's faith that looks straight at the impossibility and trusts the one
making the promise more than the evidence in front of him.

## Questions for study

1. Why does Paul go all the way back to Abraham instead of arguing from
   Moses or the prophets?
2. What's the difference between wages "owed" and righteousness
   "credited" — and why does that distinction matter so much to Paul?
3. Abraham "grew strong through faith" while staring at genuine
   impossibility. What does that kind of faith look like in your current
   situation?
4. If Abraham was justified before circumcision, what does that say about
   which comes first — belief or the practices that are supposed to
   demonstrate it?
