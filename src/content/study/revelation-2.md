Ephesus is the church that got almost everything right — hard work, patient endurance, zero tolerance for false apostles — and it's the first one Jesus corrects. Doctrinal precision and tireless labor, it turns out, aren't enough to cover for a heart that's cooled.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 2`

## Ephesus: right doctrine, lost affection

The rebuke isn't about what Ephesus believes; it's about what they no longer feel.

> *"I have this against you, that you left your first love."* — Revelation 2:4

## Smyrna: no rebuke at all

Of the seven churches, Smyrna is one of only two Jesus doesn't correct at all — and it's a church under real pressure, facing poverty and coming imprisonment. Jesus doesn't soften the hardship ahead, but he doesn't add blame to it either.

> *"Be faithful to death, and I will give you the crown of life."* — Revelation 2:10

## Pergamum and Thyatira: tolerance as the failure

Both churches get commended for something, then confronted for the same underlying problem: they've let false teaching operate inside the church rather than confronting it, mistaking tolerance for love.

## Questions for study

1. Why might diligence and doctrinal correctness not be enough on their own, as Ephesus discovers?
2. What does it mean that Smyrna, the church facing the worst circumstances, receives no correction at all?
3. Where's the line between patience with people and tolerance of what's actually harmful, as Pergamum and Thyatira illustrate?
4. Which of these four churches' specific commendation or warning lands closest to your own situation?
