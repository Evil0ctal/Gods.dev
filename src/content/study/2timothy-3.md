Paul's list of end-times behavior in 3:1-5 reads less like prophecy and more like a description of people everybody already knows — lovers of self, lovers of money, boastful, unforgiving, "holding a form of godliness but having denied its power" (3:5). It's less interested in predicting a calendar than in naming a pattern that recurs whenever it recurs.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 2timothy 3`

## Religion with the plug pulled

That phrase — a form of godliness, power denied — is the chapter's sharpest line. It describes people who keep the shape of faith, the words and postures and maybe the attendance, without the thing the shape was built to carry. Paul's instruction isn't to argue with them; it's to "turn away" (3:5).

## Persecution as a given, not an anomaly

Verse 12 states something Paul doesn't apologize for: "all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution." Paired with Paul's own résumé of suffering at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra (3:11), this isn't despair — it's calibration. Expecting smooth passage is the miscalculation; expecting resistance is realism.

## What you already know

Against deceivers who will "grow worse and worse" (3:13), Paul's counsel to Timothy is oddly unspectacular: stay with what you learned, remember who taught it to you (3:14). Then the chapter's other famous line:

> *"Every Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness."* — 2 Timothy 3:16

Not a claim about power to impress — a claim about usefulness, spelled out in four specific functions.

## Questions for study

1. Where have you seen "a form of godliness" with the power missing — in yourself or elsewhere?
2. Does 3:12 change how you interpret hardship connected to living rightly?
3. Of the four functions in 3:16 — teaching, reproof, correction, instruction — which do you resist most?
4. What have you "learned and been assured of" (3:14) that you're most tempted to abandon under pressure?
