The two halves of this chapter — head coverings and the Lord's Supper — can feel like unrelated topics stitched together, but they share a diagnosis. In both cases, something meant to express unity had become a display of status. Head coverings were tangled up with honor and visibility in worship; the communal meal that was supposed to precede the Lord's Supper had turned into some eating well while others, arriving late from work, went hungry (11:20-21).

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1corinthians 11`

## A meal that had stopped being one meal

Paul's anger in verses 17-22 is pointed: "when you come together, it is not to eat the Lord's supper" — meaning whatever was happening technically resembled the ritual but had lost its substance. The wealthy weren't waiting for the poor; the "one body" language of unity was being contradicted at the very meal meant to enact it.

## The words behind the ritual

> *"For as often as you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes."* — 1 Corinthians 11:26

Communion here isn't private devotion — it's a public act with a horizon (until he comes) and a content (the Lord's death). Doing it while ignoring hungry members at your own table empties the proclamation of what it claims to announce.

## Wait for each other

> *"Therefore, my brothers, when you come together to eat, wait for one another."* — 1 Corinthians 11:33

The remedy Paul lands on is almost embarrassingly simple: eat at home first if you're that hungry, but don't let the shared meal become a place where some are shamed and others are stuffed.

## Questions for study

1. Where in your own gatherings has form outlasted the substance it was meant to express?
2. What would it mean, practically, to "wait for one another" in a shared meal or shared resource today?
3. How does knowing communion "proclaims the Lord's death" change how you'd want to take it?
