The writer makes an almost mathematical argument here: a sacrifice that has to be offered every single year is, by its own structure, a sacrifice that never actually worked. If it had removed sin the first time, the worshipers would have had no more consciousness of sin and no reason to return. The repetition isn't devotion — it's the system admitting its own limits.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible hebrews 10`

## Sitting down versus standing daily

> *"Every priest indeed stands day by day serving and offering... but Christ, having offered one sacrifice for sins forever, sat down at the right hand of God."* — Hebrews 10:11-12 (paraphrased)

That posture change — standing to serve, sitting once finished — is the writer's shorthand for a completed work versus an endless one.

## Sanctified through one offering

> *"By this will, we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all."* — Hebrews 10:10

## What that finished work asks of you now

The exhortation that follows isn't passive gratitude — draw near with a true heart, hold fast the confession of hope, and consider one another so as to provoke love and good works, "not forsaking our own assembling together, as the custom of some is."

> *"But the righteous will live by faith. If he shrinks back, my soul has no pleasure in him."* — Hebrews 10:38

## Questions for study

1. Why would a sacrifice needing constant repetition count as evidence against its own effectiveness?
2. What does the image of Christ "sitting down" suggest about the state of his work?
3. What would it look like this week to "provoke one another to love and good works" rather than simply coexist?
