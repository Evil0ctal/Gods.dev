Picture the scene Paul is regulating: multiple people speaking in tongues at once, no interpretation offered, visitors walking in bewildered (14:23). It's not that tongues were fake or unwelcome — Paul says he uses the gift more than anyone (14:18) — it's that a worship service optimized for the speaker's experience rather than the listener's understanding had stopped functioning as a gathering at all.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1corinthians 14`

## The test is intelligibility, not sincerity

> *"So also you, since you are zealous for spiritual gifts, seek that you may abound to the building up of the assembly."* — 1 Corinthians 14:12

Paul's repeated standard throughout the chapter isn't "is this gift real" but "does this build others up." Five intelligible words that instruct someone are worth more, he says bluntly, than ten thousand words no one can understand (14:19). Enthusiasm isn't the same thing as edification.

## Order isn't the opposite of the Spirit

> *"For God is not a God of confusion, but of peace."* — 1 Corinthians 14:33

It would be easy to read regulations on speaking order as institutional caution smothering spontaneity. Paul frames it the other way: chaos isn't evidence of the Spirit's presence, and structure isn't evidence of the Spirit's absence. A God of peace produces gatherings that make sense to the people in them.

## Let all things be done decently

The chapter's closing instruction — "let all things be done decently and in order" (14:40) — sounds almost administrative after everything before it, but it's the practical conclusion of the whole argument: gifts are for the room, not just for the person exercising them.

## Questions for study

1. Where might your own enthusiasm for something be outpacing its usefulness to the people around you?
2. How do you tell the difference between quenching the Spirit and providing helpful order?
3. Is there a gift or contribution you make that would land better with more explanation, not less?
