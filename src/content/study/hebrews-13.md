After eleven chapters of high argument — Jesus better than angels, better than Moses, a better priest under a better covenant — the letter ends almost abruptly practical. Keep loving each other. Don't neglect hospitality to strangers. Remember prisoners as though chained with them. Honor marriage. Stay free of money's grip. Whatever "better" has meant through this whole letter, it has to land somewhere as ordinary as who you let sleep in your house.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible hebrews 13`

## Hospitality with a hidden stake

> *"Don't forget to show hospitality to strangers, for in doing so, some have entertained angels without knowing it."* — Hebrews 13:2

The instruction doesn't rest on the guest turning out to be impressive. It rests on not knowing in advance who they are.

## Contentment as a countermeasure

> *"Be free from the love of money, content with such things as you have, for he has said, 'I will in no way leave you, neither will I in any way forsake you.'"* — Hebrews 13:5

The cure for money's grip isn't offered as willpower — it's a promise substituted for a fear.

## The one constant underneath it all

> *"Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever."* — Hebrews 13:8

Coming right after instructions about leaders and strange teachings, this verse functions as ballast: practices and personalities shift, but the center doesn't move.

## Questions for study

1. Why base hospitality on not knowing who a stranger is, rather than on their apparent worthiness?
2. What promise would need to feel real to you before contentment stopped being a strain?
3. How does Christ's unchanging nature steady you specifically where you feel unsettled right now?
