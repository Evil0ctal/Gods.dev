Chapter 4 is the hinge of the letter. Everything before it describes what God has done; everything after it describes how to live in light of that. Paul marks the turn with a single word he'll repeat across three chapters: "walk."

> Read it in the terminal: `bible ephesians 4`

## Seven "ones" and a body full of parts

Verses 4-6 stack up a sevenfold unity — one body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father — and then pivot straight into diversity: different gifts, given to build up different parts of the same body (4:11-13). Unity, in Paul's picture, isn't sameness. It's one structure with many functions.

## Put off, put on

The back half of the chapter gets concrete: stop lying, don't let anger curdle into sin overnight, the thief should work instead of steal, speech should build up instead of tear down (4:25-31). This isn't abstract virtue. It reads like a list Paul actually expected people to apply by Friday.

## The last line as the whole point

> *"And be kind to one another, tender hearted, forgiving each other, just as God also in Christ forgave you."* — Ephesians 4:32

Everything in the chapter's second half collapses into this one instruction, and the stated reason is not "because it works" but "because you were forgiven first."

## Questions for study

1. Where does the chapter's picture of unity-through-difference challenge how you think about unity?
2. Which "put off, put on" pair in 4:25-32 is hardest for you right now?
3. How does being forgiven first change the difficulty of forgiving someone else?
