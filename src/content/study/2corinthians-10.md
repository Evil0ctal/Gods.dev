Someone in Corinth had a line about Paul, and he quotes it back to them:
"his letters are weighty and strong, but his bodily presence is weak,
and his speech is despised" (10:10). It's a devastating insult — you
write like a heavyweight and show up like nobody. Rather than deny it,
Paul reframes the entire contest. If you're grading him on stage
presence, he says, you're grading the wrong thing.

## Not a war of appearances

*"For the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh, but mighty
before God to the throwing down of strongholds, throwing down
imaginations and every high thing that is exalted against the knowledge
of God, and bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of
Christ."* — 2 Corinthians 10:4-5

The military language is deliberate. Corinth's culture ran on rhetorical
performance — impressive speech, impressive presence. Paul says his real
combat happens somewhere else: strongholds of thought, not stages of
delivery. A stronghold, notably, is not a person; it's a fortified
argument, an "imagination" that has walled itself off from truth. That's
what his weapons are built to demolish — not opponents, but the mental
fortresses opponents hide inside.

## Boasting recalibrated

The chapter ends on measurement: Paul refuses to compare himself to
others "by their own selves" (10:12), calling that comparison "not
wise." His only acceptable boast is borrowed: "he who boasts, let him
boast in the Lord" (10:17), because "not he who commends himself is
approved, but whom the Lord commends" (10:18).

## Questions for study

1. Where do you find yourself grading people — or yourself — on the
   wrong scoreboard?
2. What "stronghold" of thought in your own mind might need demolishing
   rather than defending?
3. Whose approval are you currently chasing that Paul would call the
   wrong court entirely?
