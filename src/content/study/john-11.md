Mary and Martha send a message with no request attached, just information: "Lord, behold, he for whom you have great affection is sick" (11:3). They assume that's enough. Jesus reads it, and then does something the sisters could not have expected — he stays where he is for two more days. John doesn't soften the delay; he reports it flatly, right after reminding us that Jesus loved this family (11:5-6).

> Read it in the terminal: `bible john 11`

## The delay

By the time Jesus arrives, Lazarus has been in the tomb four days — long enough, in the culture's reckoning, to remove any doubt about whether he was really dead. The delay wasn't neglect; it was making room for something larger than a rescue.

## "I am the resurrection"

Martha meets him first, half-faith and half-complaint: if he'd been here, her brother wouldn't have died. Jesus doesn't answer with sympathy alone. He answers with a claim about himself:

> *"I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in me will still live, even if he dies."* — John 11:25

## Jesus wept

Then, at the tomb, the shortest verse in the book: *"Jesus wept"* (11:35). He is about to call Lazarus out in a matter of moments; he weeps anyway. Knowing the ending doesn't cancel the grief in the middle of the story — a detail worth sitting with. Then comes the call: *"Lazarus, come out!"* (11:43) — and a dead man walks out, still wrapped in graveclothes.

## Questions for study

1. What do you do with a delay from God that you cannot yet explain?
2. Why might Jesus weep at a tomb he's about to empty?
3. Martha's declaration of faith (11:27) comes before the miracle — what does that say about the order of belief and evidence?
4. The chapter ends with the authorities plotting Jesus' death because of this sign (11:45-53). What does it cost to raise someone from the dead?
