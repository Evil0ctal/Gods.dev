Paul wants the Corinthians to see him and Apollos as something specific: Christ's servants, "and stewards of God's mysteries" (4:1). A steward manages someone else's household — the job description implies you don't get to grade the steward on flair, only on whether he handled what wasn't his own with integrity: "it is required of stewards, that they be found faithful" (4:2).

## Who are you to judge?

Paul pushes back hard against a congregation that had apparently started ranking its leaders like a talent show. "who makes you different? And what do you have that you didn't receive?" (4:7) is one of the more deflating questions in the letter — every gift you're proud of was handed to you, which makes pride in it a strange kind of accounting error.

## The apostolic résumé nobody wants

Then Paul gets sarcastic, contrasting the Corinthians' self-satisfaction — "you have come to reign without us" (4:8) — with what apostleship actually looked like from the inside.

> *"Even to this present hour we hunger, thirst, are naked, are beaten, and have no certain dwelling place."* — 1 Corinthians 4:11

It's not humility performed for effect; it's a plain description of the actual cost.

## A father, not just a teacher

The chapter ends tender rather than combative — Paul as a father who "became your father through the Good News" (4:15), asking them to imitate him, not just admire him from a safe distance.

## Questions for study

1. Where might you be grading spiritual leaders on charisma rather than faithfulness?
2. "What do you have that you didn't receive?" — apply that question to whatever you're currently proud of.
3. How does Paul's résumé in verses 8-13 compare to what you'd list as evidence of a "successful" ministry today?
