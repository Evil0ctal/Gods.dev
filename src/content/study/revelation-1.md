John tells us exactly where he is when this book begins: on the island of Patmos, "because of God's Word and the testimony of Jesus Christ" (v.9) — a phrasing that reads a lot like exile, not vacation. Revelation doesn't open with abstract prophecy; it opens with a specific man, in a specific and unpleasant place, on the Lord's day, when he turns around and sees something that levels him.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 1`

## A description built to overwhelm

What John sees isn't a gentle figure. Hair white as wool, eyes like blazing fire, feet like polished bronze, a voice like rushing water, a sword coming from his mouth, a face like the sun at full strength. It's not a portrait meant to be pieced together calmly; it's meant to do what it does to John.

> *"When I saw him, I fell at his feet like a dead man."* — Revelation 1:17

## The hand on the shoulder

What follows the collapse matters as much as the vision itself. The same figure who just overwhelmed John touches him and speaks the words that anchor the whole book.

> *"Don't be afraid. I am the first and the last, and the Living one. I was dead, and behold, I am alive forever and ever."* — Revelation 1:17-18

## Questions for study

1. Why might Revelation open with John's exile rather than skipping straight to the vision?
2. What does John's reaction — falling down "like a dead man" — tell you about how this vision was meant to land?
3. Why pair an overwhelming vision with the immediate reassurance "don't be afraid"?
