Someone brings Jesus news of Galileans killed by Pilate while offering sacrifices — a horror looking for a theological explanation. Jesus doesn't give one. Instead he asks whether the crowd thinks the victims were worse sinners than anyone else for having suffered that way, and answers his own question: no, but unless you repent, you'll perish just as certainly. He adds a second tragedy, a tower collapse, and repeats the same non-explanation.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible luke 13`

## A gardener buys time

The parable that follows fits the mood exactly: a fig tree has produced nothing for three years, and the owner wants it cut down. The gardener asks for one more year to dig around it and fertilize it. The parable ends without telling us whether the fruit ever comes — it just holds open a window most people would have already closed.

## A door that narrows

Asked whether only a few will be saved, Jesus doesn't answer the head-count question directly. He talks instead about a narrow door that many will try and fail to enter once it's shut, and people who assumed familiarity with Jesus discovering it wasn't enough.

## Grief over a city

The chapter ends with Jesus lamenting over Jerusalem like someone who has already lost the argument he wishes he hadn't had to have — a hen gathering chicks that keep refusing to be gathered.

> *"Lord, leave it alone this year also, until I dig around it, and fertilize it."* — Luke 13:8

> *"How often I wanted to gather your children together... and you refused!"* — Luke 13:34

## Questions for study

1. Why does Jesus refuse to explain why the Galileans died the way they did?
2. What does the gardener's request for one more year assume about the tree?
3. Why does Jesus redirect a question about numbers into a question about urgency?
4. What does the lament over Jerusalem reveal about how Jesus views rejection?
