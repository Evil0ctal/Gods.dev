A man with a withered hand stands in the synagogue, and the room is watching Jesus "that they might accuse him" (3:2) more than watching the man in need. Jesus puts the actual question to them plainly: *"Is it lawful on the Sabbath day to do good, or to do harm? To save a life, or to kill?"* (3:4). Nobody answers. Mark doesn't soften the moment — he says Jesus looked at them "with anger, being grieved at the hardening of their hearts" (3:5), and healed the man anyway, silence or not.

## Grief that doesn't wait for permission

That combination — anger and grief in the same verse — is worth noticing. Jesus isn't merely irritated at being obstructed; he's grieved at what the silence reveals, hearts so committed to a position that a man's hand mattered less than winning an argument. He heals the man in front of everyone, refusing to let the withheld answer withhold the mercy.

## Twelve names, one purpose

From the growing crowds, Jesus deliberately appoints twelve, "that they might be with him, and that he might send them out" (3:14) — the sequence matters: presence before mission, formation before assignment.

## An accusation, and a family redefined

Scribes accuse Jesus of casting out demons by the prince of demons; he answers that a divided kingdom can't stand, and warns about a sin against the Holy Spirit that goes unforgiven (3:22-30) — not a casual line, but a warning against calling good things evil so persistently that you lose the capacity to recognize good at all. When his own family arrives to restrain him, thinking him out of his mind, Jesus redefines the category entirely: *"Whoever does the will of God, the same is my brother, and my sister, and mother"* (3:35).

## Questions for study

1. What would it mean to hold both anger and grief toward someone's hardness of heart, the way Jesus does in 3:5?
2. The twelve were called to "be with him" before being sent out. Where in your life has assignment come before formation, and what did that cost?
3. What's the danger, practically, in getting so committed to a position that you'd rather stay silent than admit good is happening in front of you?
4. How does Jesus' redefinition of family in 3:35 challenge or comfort you?
