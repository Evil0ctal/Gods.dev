Every other Pauline letter opens with thanks. This one skips straight to
alarm: "I marvel that you are so quickly deserting him who called you...
and are turning to a different 'good news'" (1:6). No warm-up, no
compliment, no pastoral throat-clearing — just astonishment, fast enough
that you can tell Paul wrote this one hot.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible galatians 1`

## A curse, twice

Verses 8-9 escalate immediately: "if we, or an angel from heaven, preach
to you any 'good news' other than that which we preached to you, let him
be cursed." He says it twice, almost identically, which in ancient
rhetoric is not redundancy — it's emphasis by repetition, the equivalent
of underlining a sentence and then underlining it again. Whatever the
Galatians were being told, Paul treats it as an emergency, not a
variation worth debating politely.

## Not from man

The rest of the chapter is autobiography with a purpose: Paul insists
his gospel came "through revelation of Jesus Christ" (1:12), not from any
human committee. He recounts his former life persecuting the church "and
tried to destroy it" (1:13), then his years away from Jerusalem before
ever meeting the apostles there. The point isn't ego — it's that his
authority doesn't rest on human transmission, so a human argument can't
simply overrule it.

*"For neither did I receive it from man, nor was I taught it, but it
came to me through revelation of Jesus Christ."* — Galatians 1:12

## Questions for study

1. Why might Paul consider this situation urgent enough to skip
   thanksgiving and open with a curse?
2. Is there a version of "good news" in your own circles that sounds
   close to the real thing but subtly changes its terms?
3. Paul's authority rests on revelation, not committee approval. Where
   do you rest your own convictions, and does that source hold up under
   pressure?
