Chapter 9 ended in tension — election, mercy, a stumbling stone. Chapter 10 answers with a door thrown open: whoever calls on the name of the Lord will be saved (10:13). Paul isn't backing off what he said about sovereignty; he's insisting sovereignty and open invitation were never in competition.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible romans 10`

## Zeal without knowledge

Paul's diagnosis of Israel is precise and painful: "they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge" (10:2). It's possible to be sincerely, exhaustingly religious and still be building the wrong thing — seeking to establish your own righteousness instead of receiving one already offered (10:3). Sincerity was never the missing ingredient.

## The chain of verse 14

Paul builds a chain backward from calling to hearing to preaching to sending, and it's worth reading slowly:

> *"How then will they call on him in whom they have not believed? How will they believe in him whom they have not heard? How will they hear without a preacher?"* — Romans 10:14

Faith isn't a private achievement that happens in a vacuum. Somewhere in the chain, someone spoke, and someone heard. That makes proclamation — unglamorous, ordinary talking — the hinge Paul refuses to treat as optional.

## Faith comes by hearing

The chapter's most quoted line follows naturally: faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God (10:17). Not by feeling, not by proof, not by achievement — by a word received.

## Questions for study

1. Where have you mistaken sincerity or effort for the righteousness Paul says can only be received?
2. Who was the "preacher" in verse 14 for you — the ordinary person whose words became the hearing that led to your faith?
3. Verse 13 says "whoever." How wide do you actually let that word stretch in practice?
