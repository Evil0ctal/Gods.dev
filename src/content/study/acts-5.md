Peter's question to Ananias contains the whole point of the story, and it's easy to miss on a fast read: *"While you kept it, didn't it remain your own? After it was sold, wasn't it in your power?"* (5:4). Nobody required this couple to sell their land, and nobody required them to give all the proceeds. The sin wasn't holding some money back. It was pretending they hadn't.

## A performance of generosity

Ananias and Sapphira wanted the reputation of total sacrifice (like Barnabas in the previous chapter) without the actual cost. Luke calls this lying "to the Holy Spirit," not just to the apostles — the community's honesty with each other and its integrity before God are treated as the same thing.

## Gamaliel's wager

Later, a different kind of caution appears. Facing apostles the council wants dead, the Pharisee Gamaliel counsels patience:

> *"If this counsel or this work is of men, it will be overthrown. But if it is of God, you will not be able to overthrow it, and you would be found even to be fighting against God!"* — Acts 5:38-39

It's not exactly faith, but it's not nothing either — a willingness to let time be the judge rather than force.

## Dishonor as a badge

The chapter's last line reframes suffering entirely: the apostles leave "rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for Jesus' name" (5:41). Beaten and warned, their response isn't grim endurance but something closer to honor.

## Questions for study

1. Why is a lie about generosity worse, in this story, than simply giving less?
2. What's the difference between Gamaliel's wait-and-see caution and real faith?
3. What would it take for you to experience dishonor as something to rejoice in, rather than merely survive?
4. How does this chapter's fear (5:5, 5:11) sit next to its boldness (5:41-42)?
