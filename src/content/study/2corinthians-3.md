Paul opens defensively — apparently someone had asked whether he needed letters of recommendation to prove his ministry legitimate — and pivots into one of his boldest contrasts: a ministry written on stone tablets versus one written on human hearts (3:2-3). The difference isn't just medium. It's what each covenant can actually produce in the people living under it.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 2corinthians 3`

## What the letter can't do

> *"Who also made us sufficient as servants of a new covenant; not of the letter, but of the Spirit. For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life."* — 2 Corinthians 3:6

This isn't a swipe at the law's content so much as its limits — a written code can specify what's required without supplying any power to meet it. Paul's claim for the new covenant is specifically that it doesn't just instruct, it enables.

## The veil, then and now

Paul reaches for Moses, whose face shone after meeting with God and who veiled it afterward (Exodus 34) — and reinterprets the veil as more than modesty. In Paul's reading, it also kept Israel from watching a fading glory disappear. The old covenant's glory, real as it was, had an expiration built in.

## Freedom, and being changed by looking

> *"Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty."* — 2 Corinthians 3:17

And just after it: believers, with unveiled faces, are "transformed into the same image from glory to glory" simply by beholding the Lord (3:18). The contrast with Moses is total — instead of glory that fades and needs covering, Paul describes a glory that increases the longer you look at it, changing the viewer in the process.

## Questions for study

1. Where in your life have you experienced a rule telling you what to do without giving you any power to do it?
2. What would it look like practically to spend more time "beholding" rather than just obeying?
3. Is there a glory in your own life or ministry that you suspect is fading, and how does this chapter reframe that?
