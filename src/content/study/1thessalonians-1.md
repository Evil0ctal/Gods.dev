Paul opens this letter with an odd compliment: he doesn't need to tell anyone about the Thessalonians' faith, because everyone already knows. "From you the word of the Lord has sounded forth... so that we need not to say anything" (1:8). A church only months old had already become a story other churches were telling.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1thessalonians 1`

## Three words, one memory

Paul says he remembers, without ceasing, their "work of faith and labor of love and patience of hope" (1:3) — three abstract nouns, each paired with a concrete one. Faith isn't just believed, it works. Love isn't just felt, it labors. Hope isn't just held, it endures. The pairing is the whole letter in miniature: nothing here stays theoretical for long.

## From idols to waiting

The chapter's other memory is conversion itself — how they turned to God from idols to serve a living and true God. Thessalonica was a busy trade city thick with temples; turning was not a private preference but a visible, costly reversal, done "with much affliction" and "joy of the Holy Spirit" (1:6) in the very same sentence — two words that don't usually keep company.

> *"how you turned to God from idols, to serve a living and true God, and to wait for his Son from heaven"* — 1 Thessalonians 1:9-10

## Questions for study

1. What would "work of faith, labor of love, patience of hope" look like translated into your actual week?
2. Is your faith currently the kind of story that "sounds forth" without you having to explain it?
3. Affliction and joy appear together in verse 6. Where have you experienced both at once?
4. What idols — not statues, but competing loyalties — did your own turning require leaving behind?
