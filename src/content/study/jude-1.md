Jude says outright that he sat down to write about "the salvation we share" and changed his mind mid-thought, because "certain men crept in unnoticed" among his readers, turning grace into an excuse for immorality. The letter he ended up sending isn't the one he planned. That's worth sitting with — sometimes an urgent situation overrides the letter you meant to write.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible jude 1`

## Old stories, used as warnings

Jude reaches for history to make his case: a rescued people later destroyed for unbelief, angels who didn't keep their proper place, Sodom and Gomorrah's judgment. None of it is decoration — each example backs the same claim, that being included once doesn't guarantee safety if you abandon what you were included for.

## Contending, not just holding on

Jude doesn't ask his readers to merely preserve their faith quietly. He tells them to fight for it.

> *"Contend earnestly for the faith which was once for all delivered to the saints."* — Jude 1:3

## Kept, in the end

After all the warnings, the letter's final movement is tender rather than harsh — a call to build each other up, pray, show mercy to the doubtful, and trust God to finish what he started.

> *"Keep yourselves in the love of God, looking for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ to eternal life."* — Jude 1:21

## Questions for study

1. Why might Jude have felt he had to abandon his planned letter for this more urgent one?
2. What's the point of reaching back into old stories of judgment rather than just stating the danger directly?
3. How does "contend for the faith" differ from simply defending your own beliefs when challenged?
4. What does it mean to "keep yourself in the love of God" as something you do, not just something you feel?
