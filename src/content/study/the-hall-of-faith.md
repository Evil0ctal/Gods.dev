The chapter opens with a definition before it gives a single example: *"faith is assurance of things hoped for, proof of things not seen"* (11:1). Then, for the rest of the chapter, the writer proves the definition by rapid-fire biography — Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Moses, and a closing list too long to name individually. The refrain "by faith" repeats so often it starts to feel like a drumbeat.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible hebrews 11`

## Not a highlight reel of success

It's worth noticing what "by faith" actually covers here. Abraham obeyed "not knowing where he went." Moses's parents hid him, risking Pharaoh's decree. Others in the closing verses were tortured, mocked, imprisoned, and killed, and the text says plainly they "didn't receive the promise" (11:39) in their lifetimes. This isn't a chapter about people who got what they asked for. It's a chapter about people who kept trusting without it.

## Strangers on purpose

Several of these figures are described as confessing they were "strangers and pilgrims on the earth" (11:13), explicitly looking for a country not yet given to them. Faith, in this chapter, includes living comfortably unsettled — building a life while admitting the destination is somewhere else.

> *"Without faith it is impossible to be well pleasing to him, for he who comes to God must believe that he exists, and that he is a rewarder of those who seek him."* — Hebrews 11:6

## Questions for study

1. How does the chapter's definition of faith in 11:1 compare to how you usually use the word?
2. Why include people in the list who didn't get the outcome they hoped for?
3. What does it mean, practically, to live as a "stranger and pilgrim" where you are now?
4. Hebrews 12:1 calls this list a "cloud of witnesses." How does that image change the way you read chapter 11?
