Revelation's own opening line names what it is before any reader gets the chance to guess: "the revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave him to show to his servants" (1:1). The Greek word behind "revelation" is an unveiling — a pulling back of a curtain, not a code needing a key. Whatever else this book does with beasts, bowls, and dragons, it insists it's showing something, not hiding it.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 1`

## A structure of escalating cycles

After letters to seven churches (2-3) and a throne-room vision built around a slain Lamb found worthy to open a sealed scroll (4-5), the book moves through cycles of judgment that escalate in intensity — seven seals, seven trumpets, seven bowls — each interrupted by scenes of worship or comfort before the next wave lands. It's not a straight timeline so much as a spiral, circling the same territory with rising stakes.

## The center of gravity

> *"Worthy is the Lamb who has been killed, to receive the power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and blessing!"* — Revelation 5:12

Every plague and every beast in the chapters that follow gets its authority to exist measured against this one figure. The Lamb who was slain, not the beasts that rage, is the actual center of the book's gravity.

## Where it lands

The trilogy of dragon, beast, and false prophet (12-13) gives way to Babylon's fall (17-18), the rider on the white horse (19), a thousand years and a final judgment (20), and finally a new heaven and earth where God dwells with his people and wipes every tear away (21-22). The arc bends from unveiling wrath to unveiling a wedding.

## Questions for study

1. How does reading Revelation as an "unveiling" rather than a puzzle change what you expect from it?
2. Why structure judgment in escalating cycles rather than a single straight sequence?
3. What does it mean that the Lamb who was slain, not the beasts, is named worthy at the book's structural center?
