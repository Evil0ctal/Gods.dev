An angel rolls back the stone and sits on it, and the soldiers guarding the tomb — men whose entire job was to not be afraid — "became like dead men" (28:4). Meanwhile the two women who came to grieve are told not to be afraid, and they leave "with fear and great joy" (28:8) — afraid, and moving anyway. The chapter's first irony: the professionals collapse, and the frightened women run.

## An empty tomb, explained twice

The angel's explanation is plain: *"He is not here, for he has risen, just like he said"* (28:6). The chief priests' explanation, bought with a large bribe to the soldiers, is that the disciples stole the body while the guards slept (28:12-13) — an account that quietly concedes the tomb really was empty while explaining it away. Matthew doesn't argue with the cover story; he just reports it as a cover story, on record, still being told "until this day" (28:15).

## Fear and joy, not fear or joy

The women's mixed emotion in verse 8 is worth sitting with. The resurrection doesn't erase the fear of an empty tomb and an unprecedented event — it runs alongside real joy, not after it. Faith here isn't the absence of fear; it's carrying both.

## A commission from a mountain

The Gospel ends on a mountain in Galilee with a command and a promise braided together: make disciples of all nations, and *"behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age"* (28:20). The last word of Matthew's Gospel is not goodbye — it's presence.

## Questions for study

1. What would it look like to move forward "with fear and great joy" rather than waiting for the fear to fully clear first?
2. The cover story in 28:11-15 unintentionally confirms the empty tomb. What does that detail add to how you read the resurrection?
3. Matthew's Gospel opened with "God with us" (1:23) and closes with "I am with you always." What does that bookend do to the whole book?
4. What "nation" — a group, a relationship, a place — is your actual mission field right now?
