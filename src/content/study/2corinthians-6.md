If you asked most people to prove they were qualified for their job,
they would list achievements. Paul lists afflictions. "In much
endurance, in afflictions, in hardships, in distresses, in beatings, in
imprisonments, in riots, in labors, in watchings, in fastings" (6:4-5) —
nine or ten items deep, and he's not finished. This is his letter of
recommendation, and it reads like a casualty report.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 2corinthians 6`

## Credentials nobody wants

The logic is deliberately upside down. Paul is answering critics in
Corinth who judged ministers by polish and rhetorical flourish. He
answers with scars. Verse 10 crystallizes the paradox: "as sorrowful, yet
always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and
yet possessing all things." None of that is spin. It is the actual shape
of a life spent absorbing cost for someone else's benefit.

## Open wide

Verses 11-13 shift tone entirely — Paul stops defending and starts
pleading. "Our mouth is open to you... you are not restricted by us, but
you are restricted by your own affections." He wants reconciliation, not
vindication, and says so plainly: *open your hearts wide.*

## Don't yoke what doesn't match

*"Don't be unequally yoked with unbelievers, for what fellowship have
righteousness and iniquity?"* — 2 Corinthians 6:14

A yoke binds two animals to pull one direction at one pace. Paul's
warning isn't isolationism; it's a farming image about mismatched
partnership — the kind that strains both parties and plows a crooked
row. "Come out from among them, and be separate" (6:17) closes the
chapter with a call to a distinct, undivided loyalty.

## Questions for study

1. What would your own "list of credentials" look like if it were
   written in hardships endured rather than achievements won?
2. Where is your heart currently "restricted" — narrowed by something
   other than the people you claim to love?
3. Name one "yoke" in your life that pulls two directions at once. What
   would it take to unfasten it?
