Chapter 5 opens with a specific, ugly report: a man is sleeping with his father's wife, immorality "not even named among the Gentiles" (5:1). What stops Paul cold isn't only the act — it's the congregation's reaction. They're "puffed up," not mourning (5:2). Somewhere, tolerance had been mistaken for maturity.

## A little yeast

Paul's central image is domestic and precise: "Don't you know that a little yeast leavens the whole lump?" (5:6). Yeast doesn't stay contained to its corner of the dough — that's its nature. Paul's point isn't that this one man's sin will personally corrupt everyone; it's that a community's willingness to shrug at sin, once established as a habit, doesn't stay confined either.

## Not the world's job to judge

Paul draws a boundary that gets misquoted constantly: he isn't calling for Christians to police the outside world's morality — he'd have to leave the world entirely to avoid all its immorality (5:10). The judgment he's calling for is internal: "Don't you judge those who are within?" (5:12). This is a chapter about the church's responsibility for its own household, not a mandate to judge outsiders.

## Removed for the sake of restoration

> *"Purge out the old yeast, that you may be a new lump, even as you are unleavened."* — 1 Corinthians 5:7

The removal Paul calls for (5:13) isn't described as punishment for its own sake — earlier in the chapter he frames it as handing someone over so that, ultimately, the spirit might be saved (5:5). Hard love, aimed at restoration, not exile for its own sake.

## Questions for study

1. Where does your own community confuse tolerance with love, the way Corinth apparently did?
2. How do you hold together "don't judge outsiders" (5:10, 12-13) with "do judge within the church" without those two collapsing into hypocrisy?
3. What would loving discipline aimed at someone's restoration look like in practice, rather than as punishment?
