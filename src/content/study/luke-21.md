Right after watching rich donors drop large, visible gifts into the temple treasury, Jesus notices a widow putting in two small coins, worth almost nothing by weight. He doesn't just notice her; he ranks her ahead of everyone else in the room, and then, without quite changing the subject, starts talking about the temple itself coming down stone by stone. The building everyone assumes is permanent, and the gift everyone assumes is negligible, get their values reversed in the same breath.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible luke 21`

## What the widow actually gave
Jesus doesn't measure gifts by their total. He measures what's left over afterward.

> *"Truly I tell you, this poor widow put in more than all of them."* — Luke 21:3

## Wars, and rumors, and roofs falling in
The disciples ask when the temple will fall, and Jesus answers with a long list of wars, earthquakes, betrayals, and persecutions — not as a countdown to memorize but as a warning against being fooled by anyone claiming to have the schedule. Nations in turmoil are not, on their own, a signal to panic.

## Words that outlast stone
Everything Jesus has just predicted — the temple, the city, the political order — is exactly the kind of thing that felt indestructible to people living inside it. He says something will outlast all of it anyway.

> *"Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will by no means pass away."* — Luke 21:33

## Questions for study
1. Why does the size of the widow's gift matter less than what it cost her?
2. What is Jesus warning against in his description of wars and disasters?
3. What would it look like to "watch" without either panic or indifference?
4. What in your life do you assume is as permanent as the disciples assumed the temple was?
