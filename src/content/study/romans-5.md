Having spent four chapters arguing that righteousness comes by faith,
Paul now asks what that actually buys you, and his first answer is
disarmingly simple: "we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus
Christ" (5:1). Not peace of mind as a feeling — peace as the end of
hostilities, a settled legal state, the war actually over.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible romans 5`

## A strange chain of causation

Paul then builds an unlikely chain: "we also rejoice in our sufferings,
knowing that suffering produces perseverance; and perseverance,
proven character; and proven character, hope" (5:3-4). This isn't
stoicism dressed up in religious language. It's a claim that suffering,
run through faith, actually *manufactures* the hope it seems most likely
to destroy.

> *"But God commends his own love toward us, in that while we were yet
> sinners, Christ died for us."* — Romans 5:8

## Love proven at the worst possible moment

Verse 8 is the chapter's center of gravity: the proof of God's love isn't
that Christ died for good people, or for people who were getting better —
it's that he died "while we were yet sinners." Paul's logic runs from
lesser to greater: if reconciliation happened while we were enemies, how
much more will the ongoing relationship — being "saved by his life" — be
secured now that peace exists (5:9-10).

## Adam and Christ, two heads of two humanities

The second half of the chapter runs a sweeping comparison: one man's
disobedience brought sin and death to all; one man's obedience brings
grace and life, and "much more" than merely undoing the damage (5:15-19).
Paul isn't saying grace restores what Adam lost — he's saying it
overflows past it: "where sin abounded, grace abounded much more"
(5:20).

## Questions for study

1. What's the difference between "peace of mind" and the "peace with God"
   Paul describes — a settled legal state rather than a feeling?
2. Trace the chain in 5:3-4 against your own hardest season. Did it
   produce perseverance, or did something interrupt the chain?
3. Verse 8 measures love by timing — "while we were yet sinners." How
   does that change how you extend grace to people who haven't earned
   it?
4. "Where sin abounded, grace abounded much more." Does that feel true in
   an area of your life where sin has run deep?
