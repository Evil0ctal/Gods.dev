Luke opens Acts by looking backward before he looks forward — reminding Theophilus that his "former book" covered "all that Jesus began to do and to teach" (1:1). The word *began* is doing real work: the Gospel was only the opening act. Acts picks up mid-scene, forty days after the resurrection, with Jesus still teaching and the disciples still asking the wrong question.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible acts 1`

## The wrong question, the right answer

The disciples ask if now, finally, Jesus will "restore the kingdom to Israel" (1:6) — still thinking in terms of thrones and borders. Jesus redirects them entirely:

> *"You will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come on you. You will be witnesses to me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the uttermost parts of the earth."* — Acts 1:8

That single verse is the book's table of contents. Everything from chapter 2 to chapter 28 traces that geography, Jerusalem outward.

## Looking up, then getting to work

The disciples stand staring at the sky after the ascension until two men in white interrupt them with a gentle rebuke: this Jesus will return the same way he left — so stop staring and go wait, as instructed, for what's coming (1:10-11).

## Filling a gap

The chapter ends with unfinished business handled quietly: the Eleven cast lots to choose a replacement for Judas, restoring the number to twelve before the Spirit even arrives. Practical faithfulness in the waiting room, not just anticipation.

## Questions for study

1. The disciples ask about restoring a kingdom; Jesus answers about receiving power for witness. What's the difference between those two hopes?
2. Acts 1:8 maps the whole book. What would your own "Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, ends of the earth" look like?
3. Why interrupt the disciples' skyward gaze with a promise about the future instead of details about it?
4. What does choosing Matthias before Pentecost suggest about ordinary faithfulness while waiting on God?
