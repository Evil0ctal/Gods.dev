This is the longest recorded prayer of Jesus in the Gospels, and the strangest thing about it is who's listening: not God alone, but the eleven disciples standing right there, overhearing every word. Before the arrest, before the cross, Jesus prays out loud in front of the people he's about to leave — as if he wants them to know exactly what he's asking on their behalf.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible john 17`

## Eternal life, defined

Jesus doesn't leave "eternal life" as an abstraction to sort out later. He defines it, mid-prayer:

> *"This is eternal life, that they should know you, the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ."* — John 17:3

Not a duration. A relationship. Life measured by knowing, not by counting years.

## Praying for people he hasn't met yet

The prayer moves in three widening circles: Jesus prays for himself (17:1-5), then for the disciples in the room (17:6-19), then — remarkably — for everyone who will ever believe because of their testimony (17:20). That includes every reader of this page. He asks specifically for unity: *"that they may all be one; even as you, Father, are in me, and I in you"* (17:21).

## Kept, not removed

Jesus doesn't ask the Father to take the disciples out of the world, only to keep them from the evil one while they stay in it (17:15). The prayer isn't for an escape hatch; it's for endurance inside the very world about to kill him.

## Questions for study

1. How does defining eternal life as "knowing God" change how you'd pursue it, versus treating it as a future reward?
2. Jesus prays for unity among believers before he's even arrested. Why might that request matter so much to him?
3. What's the difference between being removed from the world's trouble and being kept through it?
4. You are explicitly included in verse 20's prayer. Does it change anything to read it that way?
