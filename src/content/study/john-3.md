Nicodemus is a Pharisee, a member of the ruling council, and he comes to Jesus at night, usually read as caution, maybe even a little shame about being seen. He opens carefully, crediting Jesus as "a teacher come from God," and Jesus responds by skipping past the compliment entirely to something Nicodemus never asked about: being born again.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible john 3`

## A teacher who doesn't understand
Nicodemus takes the phrase literally, how can a grown man re-enter his mother's womb, and Jesus doesn't soften the metaphor, he expands it.

## Wind you cannot chart
Rather than explaining the mechanism, Jesus compares the Spirit's work to something nobody can track or predict.

> *"The wind blows where it wants to, and you hear its sound, but don't know where it comes from and where it is going."* — John 3:8

It isn't an evasion. It's the actual answer: this isn't a process you can manage, only one you can be caught up in.

## The verse everyone knows, and the sentence after it
John 3:16 gets quoted constantly, sometimes divorced from its context in this exact, careful, nighttime conversation.

> *"For God so loved the world, that he gave his one and only Son."* — John 3:16

Later in the chapter, John the Baptist learns that Jesus's ministry is eclipsing his own, and his response is remarkably free of resentment: he must increase, but I must decrease.

## Questions for study
1. What might Nicodemus's nighttime visit suggest about his position and his caution?
2. Why does Jesus answer with an image, wind, instead of a mechanism?
3. How does John the Baptist's response to being eclipsed model something different from rivalry?
4. Where in your life have you tried to manage something that only works if you let go of control?
