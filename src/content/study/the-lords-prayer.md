Count the words. Jesus is asked, implicitly, how to pray, and the model he gives fits in about fifty words (6:9-13) — shorter than most people's grocery lists, let alone the volumes of theology and devotional writing built on top of it since. Whatever this prayer is doing, it isn't trying to be comprehensive. It's trying to be a pattern small enough to actually use.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible matthew 6`

## Address before request

> *"Our Father in heaven, may your name be kept holy."* — Matthew 6:9

Before anything is asked for, the prayer establishes who's being addressed and puts his name's holiness ahead of any personal need. Every request that follows is downstream of that opening posture — this isn't a wish list handed to a genie, it's a conversation opened with someone specific.

## Daily, not stockpiled

> *"Give us today our daily bread."* — Matthew 6:11

The request is scaled to a day, not a season or a lifetime. There's no ask here for security in the form of surplus — just enough for today, which quietly assumes there will be a need to ask again tomorrow, and that asking again is fine.

## A forgiveness with a hinge

"Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors" (6:12) ties two forgivenesses together in a single sentence, with no space between them for delay. The prayer doesn't let you ask for mercy while withholding it elsewhere in the same breath.

## Questions for study

1. What does the prayer's brevity suggest about what it's actually meant to be used for?
2. Why does the prayer establish God's name and kingdom before any personal request appears?
3. How comfortable are you praying for "daily" provision rather than long-term security?
4. What changes if you take 6:12's forgiveness clause literally, this week, toward someone specific?
