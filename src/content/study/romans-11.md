Three chapters into a hard argument about Israel's rejection of the gospel, Paul asks the question the whole section has been building toward: did God reject his people? "May it never be!" (11:1). His proof is autobiographical — he himself is an Israelite, and he isn't rejected. The remnant isn't a technicality; it's Paul's own résumé.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible romans 11`

## Grafted, not native

Paul's central image is horticultural and, for a Gentile audience feeling smug, deliberately deflating: some natural branches were broken off, and a wild olive branch — that's you, Gentile reader — was grafted in among them (11:17). Whatever confidence Gentile believers might build on being "in," Paul cuts it off at the ankles: you don't support the root, but the root supports you (11:18).

## Do not be arrogant

The warning that follows is aimed squarely at spiritual pride: don't be conceited, but fear, because if the natural branches were broken off for unbelief, the same can happen again (11:20-21). This is Paul at his most pastorally blunt — grace received is not grace earned, and it's not a status to lord over anyone.

## A doxology, not a diagram

The chapter refuses to resolve into a tidy chart. It resolves into worship:

> *"Oh the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and the knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past tracing out!"* — Romans 11:33

## Questions for study

1. Where might you be treating your standing with God as something earned rather than something grafted in?
2. How does the warning in verses 20-21 sit with you — as fear, humility, or both?
3. Why might Paul end an argument about election with worship instead of a conclusion?
