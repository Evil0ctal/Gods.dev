The chapter opens with a conditional built entirely on the previous chapter's imagery of dying and rising with Christ in baptism: if you were raised, then seek what's above. Within a few verses, that same lofty instruction lands on a very unglamorous vice list — anger, slander, lying, malice.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible colossians 3`

## Seeking above, dealing with below

"If then you were raised together with Christ, seek the things that are above" (3:1) sounds mystical until verse 5 turns it practical: "put to death" whatever in you still belongs to the old arrangement. The chapter refuses to let cosmic identity stay abstract — it goes straight for specific behavior within a sentence or two.

## Off with the old, on with the new

> *"Put on therefore, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, a heart of compassion, kindness, lowliness, humility, and perseverance... Above all these things, walk in love, which is the bond of perfection."* — Colossians 3:12, 14

The clothing metaphor matters: you don't wait to feel compassionate before acting compassionate, any more than you wait to feel like wearing a coat before putting one on. The virtues are given as things to put on, not just feelings to develop.

## Whatever you do

The chapter closes by extending this all the way into ordinary work: "whatever you do, in word or in deed" is to be done "in the name of the Lord Jesus" (3:17), and later, to work "heartily, as for the Lord" (3:23). Even unglamorous labor gets folded into the same identity as the cosmic Christ of chapter 1.

## Questions for study

1. What in you still needs to be "put to death," per verse 5 — not managed, but ended?
2. Which virtue in 3:12 do you need to put on today, whether or not you feel it yet?
3. How do 3:17 and 3:23 change how you think about the most boring task on your list this week?
