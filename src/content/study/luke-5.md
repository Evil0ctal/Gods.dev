Peter and his partners have been at it all night and have nothing to show for it — nets clean, arms tired. Jesus, a carpenter's son with no fishing credentials, tells him to push out and let down the nets again, in daylight, after the fish should already be gone. The catch is so large the nets start to tear. Peter's reaction isn't excitement; it's terror. "Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, Lord."

## Leaving full nets on the beach

That's the strange detail worth sitting with — they don't cash in the miracle. They drag the boats to shore, loaded with the best catch of their careers, and walk away from all of it to follow Jesus. The call doesn't reward competence; it interrupts it.

## An argument over who can forgive

When Jesus tells a paralyzed man his sins are forgiven before he heals his legs, the religious leaders correctly note that only God forgives sins — they just draw the wrong conclusion from it. Jesus heals the man's legs as visible proof of an invisible claim.

## Dinner with the wrong crowd

Levi the tax collector gets called next, and throws a banquet full of his old colleagues. Jesus's answer to the complaints is blunt: he's not here for people who think they're already well.

> *"Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, Lord."* — Luke 5:8

> *"I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance."* — Luke 5:32

## Questions for study

1. Why does Peter respond to abundance with a confession of sin rather than gratitude?
2. What does it mean that the disciples leave their biggest catch behind entirely?
3. Why do the religious leaders object to Jesus forgiving sins, and what does his response prove?
4. What would it look like to throw the kind of dinner Levi throws?
