The request comes after watching, not after teaching: "when he finished praying... one of his disciples said to him, 'Lord, teach us to pray.'" Whatever they saw in him made them want that specifically, more than his miracles or his arguments with religious leaders. His answer is short — daily bread, forgiven debts, deliverance from temptation — nothing ornate about it.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible luke 11`

## A story about pounding on a door

Right after the prayer, Jesus tells a story about a man who bangs on his neighbor's door at midnight asking for bread, and gets it — not because of friendship, Jesus says, but because of sheer persistence. Ask, seek, knock: three verbs in a row, suggesting this isn't a one-time request.

## Whose finger is this by

When Jesus casts out a demon, some accuse him of doing it by the power of Beelzebul. His response is a simple logical trap — a kingdom divided against itself falls, so if Satan is casting out Satan, his kingdom is already finished. Either way, something bigger than the accusation is standing in the room.

## Woes for the meticulous

The chapter ends with Jesus condemning religious leaders who tithe garden herbs down to the leaf but neglect justice and the love of God — precision about small things covering neglect of large ones.

> *"Ask, and it will be given you. Seek, and you will find. Knock, and it will be opened for you."* — Luke 11:9

> *"How much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him!"* — Luke 11:13

## Questions for study

1. Why might the disciples have asked to be taught prayer rather than some other skill?
2. What does the midnight-neighbor story suggest about persistence in prayer?
3. What is the actual logic of Jesus's response to the Beelzebul accusation?
4. Where in your own life is precision about small things covering neglect of something larger?
