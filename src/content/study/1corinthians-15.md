Some in Corinth apparently accepted the resurrection of Jesus while doubting any future resurrection for themselves — a strange but common half-belief. Paul refuses to let the split stand. He opens by restating the gospel he'd already delivered — Christ died, was buried, was raised, was seen (15:3-5) — and then spends the rest of the chapter running the argument in reverse: if there's no resurrection at all, then Christ wasn't raised either, and if Christ wasn't raised, nothing else holds.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1corinthians 15`

## The domino, laid out plainly

> *"And if Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain, and your faith also is in vain."* — 1 Corinthians 15:14

Paul doesn't soften this. He follows it further — you're still in your sins, the dead are lost, and "we are of all men most pitiable" (15:19). It's a rare moment in scripture where an apostle states outright what would be true if the central claim were false, rather than simply asserting the claim and moving on.

## The last enemy

> *"The last enemy that will be abolished is death."* — 1 Corinthians 15:26

Death gets treated here not as a natural transition but as a hostile power finally being dismantled — which sets up the chapter's closing taunt, borrowed and repurposed from the prophets: "Death, where is your sting? Hades, where is your victory?" (15:55). It reads less like comfort and more like trash talk aimed at something Paul considers already defeated.

## Not in vain

> *"Your labor is not in vain in the Lord."* — 1 Corinthians 15:58

The chapter's final instruction is practical, not theoretical: because the resurrection is certain, ordinary effort — the unglamorous, unrewarded, easily-doubted kind — has a guarantee attached to it that nothing else can offer.

## Questions for study

1. What would genuinely change in your daily life if you ran Paul's hypothetical — no resurrection — all the way through?
2. Where do you currently treat death as more powerful than defeated?
3. What "labor" of yours has felt in vain lately, and how does 15:58 talk back to that feeling?
