When a paralyzed man is brought to Jesus, the first thing Jesus says isn't about his legs. It's "your sins are forgiven" (9:2) — and the scribes standing nearby immediately recognize the claim as blasphemous, because only God forgives sins. Jesus answers the unspoken accusation by healing the man's body as proof of the authority to heal his standing before God. The miracle is the visible half of an invisible claim.

## The tax collector at his own table

A few verses later, Jesus calls Matthew — a tax collector, a professional collaborator with Rome despised by his own people — with two words: "Follow me" (9:9). Matthew gets up and, notably, throws a dinner party, and Jesus attends alongside "many tax collectors and sinners." When the Pharisees object, Jesus gives the line that reframes his entire mission:

> *"Those who are healthy have no need for a physician, but those who are sick do... For I came not to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance."* — Matthew 9:12-13

## New wine needs new wineskins

Jesus' answer to a question about fasting introduces an image about incompatible containers: new wine will burst old, rigid wineskins (9:17). Something genuinely new has arrived and can't simply be poured into old religious categories. The chapter's healing of the woman with a chronic hemorrhage — who reaches for just the edge of his cloak, believing that alone would be enough — and the raising of Jairus's daughter both show the same pattern: desperate, unconventional faith reaching for Jesus gets met, not turned away.

## Questions for study

1. Why forgive the paralyzed man's sins before healing his body — what does that ordering claim about what the deeper problem actually is?
2. Matthew's response to being called is to throw a party for people like himself. What does that say about how the newly found ought to treat those still where they were?
3. The bleeding woman reached out from a crowd, expecting nothing more than the hem of a cloak. Where do you need that kind of desperate, minimal-but-real faith right now?
