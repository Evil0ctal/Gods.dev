John is handed a measuring reed and told to measure the temple, the altar, and the worshippers — but to leave the outer court unmeasured, because it's been handed over to the nations (11:1-2). Measuring, here, isn't architecture. It's a way of marking off what belongs to God from what's currently under someone else's control, in a scene built to prove that arrangement won't last.

## Killed in public, raised in public

The two witnesses prophesy in sackcloth for 1,260 days, are killed by the beast, and lie unburied in the street while the world celebrates their deaths with gifts exchanged like a holiday (11:9-10). Then breath enters them, they stand up in full view, and they ascend as their enemies look on. Nothing here happens quietly. The whole point is that everyone sees it.

## The announcement

> *"The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord, and of his Christ. He will reign forever and ever!"* — Revelation 11:15

The seventh trumpet doesn't describe a future transfer of power — it declares one already accomplished, past tense, sung by voices in heaven before the earth has caught up. The twenty-four elders fall down and worship, thanking God that he "has taken your great power, and reigned" (11:17).

## Questions for study

1. What does it mean that God measures some things and deliberately leaves others out?
2. Why do the two witnesses need to be publicly killed and publicly raised rather than simply vindicated privately?
3. How does it change your reading of current events to hear the kingdom announced as already won?
