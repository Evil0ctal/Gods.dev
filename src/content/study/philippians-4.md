Paul names two women in a letter meant to be read aloud to the whole church: Euodia and Syntyche, urged to agree with each other (4:2). Real conflict, real names, no vague platitude about unity in general.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible philippians 4`

## Naming names

It would have been easier, and safer, to address the disagreement in the abstract. Paul doesn't. Whatever was wrong between these two women mattered enough to put in a letter that would outlive all of them — a reminder that unity is usually a specific repair job, not a general mood.

## Anxiety's antidote

> *"In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts."* — Philippians 4:6-7

Notice the order: not "stop being anxious," but pray instead of being anxious — the emotion gets replaced by an action, not just suppressed.

## The most misquoted verse in the letter

"I can do all things through Christ, who strengthens me" (4:13) shows up on a lot of gym walls and scoreboards. Read in context, it isn't about achievement at all — Paul says it right after describing how he's learned to be content whether he's "filled" or "hungry," to "abound" or be in "need" (4:11-12). The strength in question is for contentment, not conquest.

## Questions for study

1. What specific conflict in your own community needs a named repair job rather than a general call for unity?
2. Where does prayer replace your anxiety, versus just adding guilt on top of it?
3. How does reading 4:13 as being about contentment, not achievement, change how you'd use it?
