Verses 3 through 14 of this chapter are, in the Greek, one unbroken sentence — a long exhale of praise before Paul makes a single request of anyone. He isn't building an argument yet. He's just naming what has already happened.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible ephesians 1`

## Blessed before you did anything

> *"Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ."* — Ephesians 1:3

Note the tense. Already blessed, already chosen "before the foundation of the world" (1:4). Nothing here is aspirational; the whole passage describes a status already granted, not a project underway.

## Sealed, not just signed up

Verses 13-14 add a detail easy to skim past: believers are "sealed with the promised Holy Spirit" and given a down payment on an inheritance still to come. A seal marks ownership and guarantees authenticity — this isn't a decision left to chance, it's a mark that doesn't come off.

## A prayer for better eyesight

The chapter ends with Paul praying not for more information but for "the eyes of your hearts" to be enlightened (1:18) — that his readers would actually perceive the hope, riches, and power he just spent eleven verses naming. Knowing facts about grace and seeing them are two different things.

## Questions for study

1. What changes if "blessed" is read as a completed fact rather than a future hope?
2. What would it mean, practically, for "the eyes of your heart" to be enlightened this week?
3. Where do you know the truth of verses 3-14 but haven't yet seen it?
