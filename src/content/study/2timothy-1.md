This is a letter from a man who expects to die soon, and it opens not with strategy but with tears — Paul remembering Timothy's, hoping to see him again to be "filled with joy" (1:4). Before any instruction, 2 Timothy 1 is personal: a mentor reaching for one more contact with someone he loves.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 2timothy 1`

## A faith with a family tree

Paul traces Timothy's faith back two generations — his grandmother Lois, his mother Eunice (1:5) — a small, specific detail that grounds the whole letter. This isn't abstract theology; it's a faith handed down at a kitchen table before it was ever preached from a platform.

## Not a spirit of fear

The chapter's hinge is verse 7:

> *"For God didn't give us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control."* — 2 Timothy 1:7

Paul says this because Timothy needs to hear it — he's urged to "stir up" a gift that seems to be cooling (1:6), and told twice not to be ashamed: not of the gospel, not of Paul in chains (1:8). Fear shrinks a life; the alternative Paul names isn't bravado, it's power paired with love and discipline — three things fear tends to crowd out.

## One man who wasn't ashamed

Paul singles out Onesiphorus, who "often refreshed me" and searched Rome until he found Paul in prison, "not ashamed of my chain" (1:16-17). Against a chapter that admits others in Asia turned away (1:15), one loyal, unglamorous friend gets remembered by name. Paul builds Timothy's courage by pointing at proof it's possible.

## Questions for study

1. What gift or calling in your life needs "stirring up" rather than replacing?
2. Power, love, and self-control (1:7) — which of the three do you need most right now?
3. Whose faith did you inherit, and from whom? Whose faith might you be handing down?
4. What would it cost you to be "not ashamed" the way Onesiphorus was?
