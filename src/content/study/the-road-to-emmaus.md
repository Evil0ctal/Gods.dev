Two disciples are walking away from Jerusalem, away from the empty tomb, away from the whole confusing mess, when a stranger falls in step beside them. He asks what they're discussing. They stop, "looking sad" (24:17), and unload the whole story — the crucifixion, the third-day rumors, their crushed hope — to the one person on earth who doesn't need the recap. Luke tells us plainly why: "their eyes were kept from recognizing him" (24:16).

## A Bible study before it's a dinner

Jesus doesn't reveal himself immediately. Instead he walks them back through Moses and the prophets, showing how the suffering they just watched was written into the story all along. This is worth noticing: before the disciples get the experience of recognizing Jesus, they get the discipline of understanding him. Comprehension comes before recognition.

## The moment it breaks

They press the stranger to stay for the night. At the table, he takes the bread and blesses it and breaks it and gives it to them — the same sequence of verbs from the feeding of the five thousand and the Last Supper. That's when it happens:

> *"Their eyes were opened, and they recognized him, and he vanished out of their sight."* — Luke 24:31

## Burning hearts, running feet

Only after he's gone do they name what they'd felt: hearts "burning within us" while he talked on the road. Then they don't sit with the feeling — they walk the seven miles back to Jerusalem, at night, to tell the others. Recognition that doesn't move your feet probably isn't recognition yet.

## Questions for study

1. Why do you think Luke has Jesus teach from Scripture before revealing himself, rather than the reverse?
2. Has there been a moment when you understood something about Jesus intellectually well before it became real to you?
3. What does the specific gesture, breaking bread, suggest about how we recognize Christ now?
4. The disciples' first response to recognition is to move. What should recognition move you to do?
