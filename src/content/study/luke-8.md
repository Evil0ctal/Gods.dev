The sower scatters seed without checking the soil first — path, rock, thorns, good ground all get the same handful. Jesus's explanation isn't really about farming technique; it's about what happens to the same message in different kinds of listeners, and only one of the four outcomes bears fruit.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible luke 8`

## Asleep in the boat

Jesus falls asleep during a crossing and stays asleep through a storm violent enough to swamp the boat — the disciples have to wake him themselves. His question afterward isn't "why didn't you wake me sooner" but "where is your faith?" The calm he speaks over the water is less remarkable to Luke than the fact that faith and fear were sitting in the same boat.

## A man with no name left

Across the lake, a demon-possessed man lives naked among tombs, chained and breaking chains. When Jesus asks his name, the answer is "Legion" — he's lost even that. The pigs, the panic, and the townspeople's fear afterward all point at the same thing: this kind of freedom is disruptive to more than just the man who received it.

## An interruption inside an interruption

Jairus begs Jesus to hurry to his dying daughter, and on the way a woman with a chronic bleeding disorder touches his robe from behind, hoping not to be noticed. Jesus stops anyway, in the middle of an emergency, to find her.

> *"He said to them, 'Where is your faith?'"* — Luke 8:25

> *"Daughter, cheer up. Your faith has made you well. Go in peace."* — Luke 8:48

## Questions for study

1. What determines the outcome in the parable of the sower — the seed, or the soil?
2. Why does Jesus's question after the storm focus on faith rather than fear?
3. What does it cost the town to have the demon-possessed man healed?
4. Why does Jesus stop for the woman instead of pressing on toward the more urgent-seeming emergency?
