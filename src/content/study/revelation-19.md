Chapter 18 ends in mourning over Babylon's fall; chapter 19 opens with the same event greeted by a multitude shouting "Hallelujah! Salvation, glory, power, and might, belong to our God" (19:1). Same collapse, two completely opposite crowds. What ruins one group's economy is another group's answered prayer.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 19`

## A wedding follows a funeral

> *"For the marriage of the Lamb has come, and his wife has made herself ready."* — Revelation 19:7

The transition from lament to wedding is abrupt and clearly intentional — Babylon's fall clears the ground for a different kind of announcement altogether, one about union rather than loss.

## Don't worship the messenger

John falls at the feet of the angel showing him all this, and is immediately corrected: "You must not do that! I am a fellow bondservant with you... Worship God" (19:10). Even in a vision this overwhelming, the chapter keeps redirecting attention away from every intermediary and back to God alone.

## A name no one else knows

The rider on the white horse is called Faithful and True, has a name written that "no one knows but he himself" (19:12), and also a name in plain view: "KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS" (19:16). He's simultaneously unknowable and unmissable — hidden depth and public identity in the same figure.

## Questions for study

1. What does it mean that the same event produces mourning in chapter 18 and hallelujahs in chapter 19?
2. Why does the vision correct John for worshipping the angel rather than letting the moment pass?
3. What's the significance of the rider having both a secret name and a publicly displayed one?
