Paul calls himself, in the same breath he describes a cosmic secret entrusted to him, "the very least of all saints" (3:8). That mismatch is the point of the chapter: a plan bigger than nations, carried by someone who insists on his own smallness.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible ephesians 3`

## A secret hidden in plain sight

The "mystery" Paul keeps naming (3:3-6) isn't a puzzle to solve — it's a plan that was always true and only now announced: Gentiles are "fellow heirs" with Israel, full members, not guests. What looked like a late addition was, Paul insists, the plan from the start.

## The least of all saints

That phrase in verse 8 isn't false modesty. Paul has just described being handed the "unsearchable riches of Christ" to preach — and immediately undercuts any claim to importance. The size of the calling doesn't inflate the man; if anything, it does the opposite.

## A prayer too big to finish

The chapter ends kneeling, praying that believers would be strengthened, rooted in love, and able to grasp what cannot fully be grasped:

> *"To know Christ's love which surpasses knowledge, that you may be filled with all the fullness of God."* — Ephesians 3:19

Paul prays for people to comprehend something he just called incomprehensible, and doesn't seem to see the contradiction as a problem.

## Questions for study

1. What would it look like to hold a large calling with Paul's kind of smallness?
2. Where do you treat something as knowledge to master rather than love to be filled with?
3. Read the doxology in 3:20-21. What are you currently asking for that's smaller than what it describes?
