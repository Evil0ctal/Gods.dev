Mark 8 is the hinge the whole Gospel turns on, and it announces itself with something almost comic: the disciples have already watched Jesus feed five thousand people with five loaves, and a chapter later they're in a boat worrying they forgot to bring bread (8:14-16). Jesus's response is pointed enough to sting: "Do you not yet perceive, neither understand? Are your hearts hardened?" (8:17).

> Read it in the terminal: `bible mark 8`

## A healing in two stages

Right after that rebuke, Mark tells the only healing in the Gospels that happens gradually — a blind man at Bethsaida who, after the first touch, sees "men as trees, walking" (8:24), and only after a second touch sees clearly. Placed where it is, the story reads like commentary on the disciples: sight is coming, just not all at once.

## Who do you say that I am

That sets up the chapter's center, at Caesarea Philippi:

> *"He said to them, 'But who do you say that I am?' Peter answered, 'You are the Christ.'"* — Mark 8:29

It's the correct answer, and within three verses Peter is rebuking Jesus for predicting his own death — proof that naming Jesus correctly and understanding what it costs are two different achievements.

## The cost, stated plainly

Jesus redefines the term for everyone within earshot: whoever saves his life will lose it, whoever loses it for Jesus's sake will save it (8:35). "For what does it profit a man, to gain the whole world, and forfeit his life?" (8:36) isn't a flourish; it's the ledger the rest of the book runs on.

## Questions for study

1. The disciples forgot bread after watching two miraculous feedings. What lesson have you "seen" repeatedly but still not learned?
2. The two-stage healing suggests partial sight is a real stage, not a failure. Where are you still at "men as trees, walking"?
3. Peter names Jesus correctly and misunderstands him completely in the same scene. Is that possible for you too?
4. What would it cost you, concretely, to "lose your life" this month in the sense Jesus means?
