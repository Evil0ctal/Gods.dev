John announces its own agenda near the close of the book: "these are written that you may believe" (20:31). Every other Gospel narrates; John argues. He opens not with a birth or a genealogy but a claim older than the universe — "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (1:1) — and then the sentence that undoes every category: "The Word became flesh, and lived among us" (1:14).

> Read it in the terminal: `bible john 1`

## Signs, not just miracles

John doesn't record every wonder Jesus did; he selects a handful and calls them "signs" — pointers past themselves to who Jesus is. Water to wine, a paralytic walking, thousands fed from a boy's lunch, a man born blind seeing, Lazarus raised. Each one answers a claim the surrounding chapter makes explicit: this is the bread of life, the light of the world, the resurrection and the life.

## Two halves, one turn

The book splits roughly in half: chapters 1-12, the public ministry, where Jesus performs signs before increasingly hostile crowds; chapters 13-21, the private turn, where the audience narrows to the disciples in an upper room, a garden, a tomb, a beach. The hinge is chapter 13, where John notes that "having loved his own... he loved them to the end."

## I am

Running through both halves is a set of declarations unlike anything in the other Gospels — I am the bread of life, the light of the world, the door, the good shepherd, the resurrection and the life, the way and the truth and the life, the true vine. Each one borrows language Israel reserved for God alone and applies it, without hedging, to a carpenter from Nazareth.

## Questions for study

1. John states his purpose outright (20:31). How does knowing the goal change the way you read everything that comes before it?
2. Which of John's signs strikes you as the most direct claim about who Jesus is?
3. What shifts in tone and pace between the public ministry of chapters 1-12 and the private turn of chapters 13-21?
4. The "I am" statements echo the divine name. What is John claiming by putting that language in Jesus' mouth?
