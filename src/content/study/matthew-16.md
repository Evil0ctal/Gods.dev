Jesus asks his disciples two questions in quick succession, and the gap between them matters: first, "who do people say that the Son of Man is?" — an easy question about public opinion — and then, "who do you say that I am?" (16:13, 15). Public opinion is not what he's after. He wants their own confession, and Peter gives it without hesitation.

## The confession and the church built on it

> *"You are the Christ, the Son of the living God."* — Matthew 16:16

Jesus responds by telling Peter this wasn't a conclusion he reasoned his way to — "flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my Father" (16:17) — and promises to build his church on this confession, with even the gates of Hades unable to prevail against it. It's one of the highest points in the Gospel for Peter, and it lasts about as long as it takes to turn the page.

## The same mouth, a sharp reversal

Jesus immediately begins telling the disciples plainly that he must go to Jerusalem, suffer, be killed, and be raised (16:21) — and Peter, fresh off his correct confession, takes him aside to rebuke him for it. Jesus' response is jarring: "Get behind me, Satan! You are a stumbling block to me, for you are not setting your mind on the things of God, but on the things of men" (16:23). The same disciple who spoke by divine revelation a few verses earlier is now speaking for a very human aversion to suffering.

## The cross before the crown

The chapter's final instruction applies the pattern to everyone listening, not just Peter: *"If anyone desires to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me."* Whoever tries to save their life will lose it; whoever loses it for Jesus' sake will find it. The confession of who Jesus is cannot be separated from the cost of following him.

## Questions for study

1. Peter got the confession right and the response to suffering wrong within the same conversation. Where might you be doing something similar?
2. Jesus says Peter's insight came from the Father, not from reasoning. How do you tell the difference between revelation and your own good guess?
3. "Take up his cross" was, in that culture, an image of execution, not mere inconvenience. What does that recalibrate about how you use the phrase?
