Revelation 6 ends with an unanswered question — who can withstand what's coming — and chapter 7 answers it twice, in two very different registers. First a precise number, 144,000, sealed and counted, tribe by tribe. Then, almost immediately, a crowd John can't count at all, out of every nation, tribe, people, and language, too large for the arithmetic that just carefully listed twelve thousand from each tribe.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 7`

## Counted, then countless

The pairing feels deliberate: a specific, ordered number gives way to something that overflows every attempt to measure it. Whatever the number 144,000 signifies, it isn't meant to cap the vision — it opens into something no census could hold.

> *"After this I saw a great multitude, which no man could number, out of every nation and of all tribes, peoples, and languages."* — Revelation 7:9

## Where they came from

An elder answers the obvious question about this multitude before John can ask it — not people spared hardship, but people who came through it.

> *"These are those who came out of the great suffering. They washed their robes, and made them white in the Lamb's blood."* — Revelation 7:14

## What's promised on the other side

The chapter ends with an image of relief almost domestic in its tenderness — shelter, no more hunger or thirst, and a hand wiping away every tear.

## Questions for study

1. Why might the vision move from a precise, countable number to an uncountable multitude in the same chapter?
2. What does it mean that the great multitude is described as having come "out of" suffering rather than around it?
3. Which detail of the closing promise — shelter, no more hunger, tears wiped away — speaks most directly to you right now?
