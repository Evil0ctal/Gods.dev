Moses gets a genuine compliment here — faithful in all God's house — before the writer immediately narrows it. Moses was faithful *as a servant*, someone entrusted with a household that belonged to someone else. Jesus is faithful *as a son*, the one the house actually belongs to. It isn't an insult to Moses; it's a category difference, and the rest of the chapter builds toward what that difference should mean for how urgently you listen now.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible hebrews 3`

## The house you're already living in

"Whose house we are" — the writer won't let this stay abstract. If Jesus is son over the house and you belong to it, then how you hold your confidence isn't a private spiritual mood. It's structural to the building you're standing in.

## A warning borrowed from the wilderness

The chapter pivots hard into Psalm 95, quoting God's own verdict on the exodus generation: they watched his works for forty years and still hardened their hearts, and still never entered the rest.

> *"Today, if you will hear his voice, don't harden your hearts, as in the rebellion."* — Hebrews 3:15

The unsettling detail is that their unbelief wasn't one dramatic rejection — it was daily and gradual, described as sin's "deceitfulness." That's a harder thing to watch for in yourself than a single decisive apostasy would be.

## Exhort one another, today

The remedy isn't more willpower; it's other people. "Exhort one another daily, while it is called Today" — because hardening happens slowly and often invisibly to the one it's happening to, which is exactly why it needs outside eyes.

## Questions for study

1. What's the practical difference between serving in a house and being heir to it?
2. Where have you seen unbelief work slowly, as a hardening, rather than all at once?
3. Who plays the "exhort one another daily" role in your life right now?
