Saul's persecution is meant to end the movement. Instead: "those who were scattered abroad went around preaching the word" (8:4). Luke states it almost as an aside, but it's the engine of the whole chapter — the thing designed to crush the church becomes the thing that spreads it past Jerusalem for the first time.

## Power that can't be purchased

In Samaria, Philip's preaching draws a former sorcerer named Simon, who believes and is baptized — then tries to buy the ability to give the Holy Spirit through the laying on of hands. Peter's answer is blunt: *"May your silver perish with you, because you thought you could obtain the gift of God with money!"* (8:20). It's a warning that keeps surfacing wherever religion and money get close.

## A desert road, one traveler

Then the scene narrows dramatically: an angel sends Philip away from a thriving revival to an empty road, to meet exactly one person — an Ethiopian official reading Isaiah 53 without understanding it. Philip's question is simple: *"Do you understand what you are reading?"* (8:30). The answer becomes a private, complete gospel conversation with no crowd watching.

## The question that ends it

The eunuch's own question closes the scene: *"Behold, here is water. What is keeping me from being baptized?"* (8:36). Nothing is. He goes on his way "rejoicing" — while Philip is swept off to preach in yet another town.

## Questions for study

1. How does persecution functioning as scattering seed change how you read setbacks in your own life?
2. Why does Simon's request to buy spiritual power still feel uncomfortably familiar?
3. What does it mean that God redirects Philip from a crowd to a single reader on an empty road?
4. What is "keeping" people today from following through on what they already believe?
