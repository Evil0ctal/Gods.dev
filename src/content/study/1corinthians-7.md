The Corinthians had apparently written Paul with a question ("Now concerning the things about which you wrote to me," 7:1), and whatever the exact wording, the drift is clear from his answer: some in the church were treating marriage as a spiritual liability and singleness as the higher path. Paul spends the whole chapter refusing that hierarchy while still commending singleness — a more careful position than either side of the question expected.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1corinthians 7`

## Neither status is the obstacle

Paul tells married couples not to withhold themselves from each other except by mutual agreement (7:3-5), tells the unmarried and widows that it's fine to stay as they are (7:8), and tells the newly converted not to seek divorce from an unbelieving spouse (7:12-16). The common thread: the gospel doesn't require you to change your marital status to be taken seriously.

## The line that gets missed

> *"Only, as the Lord has distributed to each man, as God has called each, so let him walk."* — 1 Corinthians 7:17

This verse anchors the chapter. Circumcision, slavery, marriage — Paul treats none of them as the real issue. The real issue is whether you're living out your calling where you are, not whether you've engineered better circumstances first.

## An urgency, not a rulebook

The "present distress" language (7:26) and "the time is short" (7:29) explain why Paul leans toward staying single if possible — not because marriage is lesser, but because he's writing to people who may be tempted to treat any earthly arrangement as permanent when nothing here is.

## Questions for study

1. Where have you assumed a different circumstance — married, single, employed, located — would make you more faithful?
2. How do verses 3-5 push back against silent resentment in a marriage?
3. What does "remain as you were called" free you from worrying about today?
