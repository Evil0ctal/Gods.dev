The letter's closing chapter is practical almost to the point of being
blunt. Restore someone caught in a fault "in a spirit of gentleness,
looking to yourself so that you also aren't tempted" (6:1) — correction
paired immediately with self-suspicion, as if Paul assumes the corrector
is one bad day from the same failure.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible galatians 6`

## Two kinds of weight

Verses 2 and 5 sit right next to each other and appear, at first read,
to contradict: "bear one another's burdens" (6:2), then "each man will
bear his own load" (6:5). The Greek actually distinguishes them — one
word for a crushing weight too heavy to carry alone, another for the
ordinary pack every person is responsible for. The instruction isn't
confused; it's precise: help with what overwhelms, own what's yours.

## The harvest is not optional

*"Don't be deceived. God is not mocked, for whatever a man sows, that he
will also reap."* — Galatians 6:7

Paul isn't threatening; he's describing a law as reliable as agriculture.
Sowing "to his own flesh" reaps corruption; sowing "to the Spirit" reaps
life (6:8). And right after the warning comes the encouragement most
people actually need: "let us not be weary in doing good, for we will
reap in due season, if we don't give up" (6:9) — the harvest is certain,
but it isn't instant.

## One boast, one mark

Paul closes with a final jab at those pushing circumcision to avoid
persecution (6:12), then his own counter-claim: "far be it from me to
boast, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ" (6:14). He mentions
"the marks of Jesus" branded on his own body (6:17) — not spiritual
metaphor, but literal scars from the beatings chronicled elsewhere. After
six chapters of argument, his final credential is simply what the gospel
has cost him.

## Questions for study

1. Where in your life have you confused a crushing burden someone needs
   help with, and an ordinary load that's actually yours to carry?
2. "We will reap in due season, if we don't give up" — what good work
   are you tempted to abandon before its harvest?
3. What would it mean, concretely, to boast in nothing except the cross
   this week?
