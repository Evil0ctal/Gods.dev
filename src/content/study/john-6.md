Five thousand men get fed from five loaves and two fish, with baskets of leftovers to spare, and the crowd's response is entirely predictable: they try to make Jesus king by force. He slips away instead. The next day they track him down across the lake, and Jesus reads the real motive out loud: they came back for more bread, not because they understood the sign.

## Free bread and its cost
Jesus reframes what they should actually be seeking.

> *"I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will not be hungry."* — John 6:35

## A hard saying
When he pushes the metaphor into talk of eating his flesh and drinking his blood, it's too much for many who had been following him. John doesn't soften the fallout: "many of his disciples went back, and walked no more with him."

## Peter's fallback answer
Jesus turns to the twelve and asks if they're leaving too. Peter's answer isn't a confident theological essay, it's closer to admitting there's nowhere else to go.

> *"Lord, to whom would we go? You have the words of eternal life."* — John 6:68

That's not the strongest possible faith. It might be the most honest kind.

## Questions for study
1. Why does Jesus withdraw rather than let the crowd make him king?
2. What's the difference between following Jesus for bread and following him for who he is?
3. Why does John record the departure of "many disciples" without softening it?
4. Is Peter's answer in verse 68 a statement of certainty or something more like resignation, and does that difference matter?
