Before the locusts of the fifth trumpet do anything else, they're given a list of things they may not touch: no grass, no tree, no green plant — only the people who don't carry God's seal on their foreheads (9:4). It's an odd note to include in a scene otherwise built for horror. Whatever else this plague is, it isn't indiscriminate; it has a target, and it has limits even the abyss can't override.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 9`

## Torment, not termination

The locusts are given power to hurt for five months, explicitly not to kill (9:5). Scorpion tails, iron breastplates, faces described as almost human — the description reads less like an animal and more like an assembled machine of dread. But the restriction matters: this trumpet is measured cruelty, not annihilation. Annihilation is still being held back.

## A king with a name

Most plagues in Revelation are anonymous forces. This one has a ruler, and John names him in both languages available to his readers:

> *"They have over them as king the angel of the abyss. His name in Hebrew is 'Abaddon,' but in Greek, he has the name 'Apollyon.'"* — Revelation 9:11

Both names mean the same thing: Destroyer. The chapter wants its readers, in every tongue, to recognize exactly what they're looking at.

## Surviving without changing

The sixth trumpet kills a third of mankind through a monstrous cavalry, and then the chapter ends on its bleakest line: the survivors "didn't repent of the works of their hands" (9:20). Judgment this large didn't produce a single reported change of heart. That's the harder plague to sit with.

## Questions for study

1. Why might God set limits on the locusts' power rather than letting them run unchecked?
2. What does it mean that a third of humanity could witness this and still not repent?
3. Where do you see "measured" rather than total judgment operating in your own life?
