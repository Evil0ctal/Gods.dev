Peter spends verses 5-7 laying out a chain — add virtue to faith, knowledge to virtue, self-control to knowledge, and so on through perseverance, godliness, brotherly affection, and love — each quality resting on the one before it like a ladder. It's an oddly methodical passage for a letter that later gets fierce about false teachers, but the order matters: Peter is describing growth as cumulative, not a single decision you make once and finish.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 2peter 1`

## Guarding against a wasted faith

Verses 8-9 warn that without these qualities a person becomes "blind, seeing only what is near," having forgotten the cleansing from past sins. Peter isn't threatening salvation itself here so much as warning against a faith that goes nowhere — present but inert, the same concern James raises from a different angle in his own letter.

## I was there

The chapter's second half turns personal and insistent: Peter says he and the others did not follow "cunningly devised fables" but were eyewitnesses of Christ's majesty, present on the holy mountain when the voice from heaven spoke (1:16-18). It's a direct, first-person claim meant to anchor everything that follows against the false teaching he's about to confront in chapter 2.

> *"We ourselves heard this voice come out of heaven, when we were with him on the holy mountain."* — 2 Peter 1:18

## Questions for study

1. Walk through the chain in verses 5-7 — which quality feels like the missing link in your own growth right now?
2. What does it look like practically to become "blind" to your own cleansing from past sins, as verse 9 warns?
3. Why might Peter need to insist so directly on his own eyewitness testimony before turning to warn about false teachers in chapter 2?
