Jesus has just told his disciples he's leaving and one of them will betray him — not exactly a calming send-off. Chapter 14 opens with him addressing the panic directly: *"Don't let your heart be troubled. Believe in God. Believe also in me"* (14:1). He answers dread with an address, not an argument: his Father's house has room enough, and he's going to prepare a place.

## The way, not just a map

Thomas asks the practical question the others were probably thinking — they don't even know where he's going, so how can they know the way? Jesus doesn't hand him directions. He hands him an identity:

> *"I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father, except through me."* — John 14:6

The way isn't a set of instructions to follow after he's gone; the way is a person to stay attached to.

## Show us the Father

Philip's request — show us the Father, and that will be enough — gets a gently exasperated reply: *"he who has seen me has seen the Father"* (14:9). After this long together, Philip still wants a vision beyond the one standing in front of him.

## A different kind of peace

The chapter ends with a promise built to survive Jesus' absence: *"Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you; not as the world gives"* (14:27). Whatever peace the world sells depends on circumstances holding steady. This one is handed over before the circumstances get worse.

## Questions for study

1. What does it mean, practically, that Jesus is "the way" rather than merely a teacher of the way?
2. Philip wanted proof beyond what he'd already seen. Where do you do the same?
3. How is the peace of 14:27 different from the mere absence of trouble?
4. Jesus promises the Counselor will come (14:16-17, 26). What does he say the Spirit's job will be?
