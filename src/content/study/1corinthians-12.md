Some gifts in Corinth had clearly become status symbols — tongues especially seem to have functioned as proof of spiritual seriousness, leaving people with quieter gifts feeling like second-tier members. Paul's response isn't to rank the gifts differently; it's to attack the whole premise of ranking. "There are various kinds of gifts, but the same Spirit" (12:4) — the source is one, even when the expressions look nothing alike.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1corinthians 12`

## No organ gets to opt out or opt everyone else out

> *"For as the body is one, and has many members, and all the members of the body, being many, are one body; so also is Christ."* — 1 Corinthians 12:12

Paul then runs the body metaphor two directions at once: the foot can't disqualify itself for not being a hand (12:15-16), and the eye can't dismiss the hand as unnecessary (12:21). Insecurity and superiority turn out to be the same error wearing different clothes.

## Suffering and honor are shared property

> *"When one member suffers, all the members suffer with it. Or when one member is honored, all the members rejoice with it."* — 1 Corinthians 12:26

This is the verse that quietly does the most work. It rules out both indifference to another member's pain and envy at another member's honor — neither is available to a body that actually functions as one.

## A more excellent way, coming next

The chapter ends on a genuine ranking ("earnestly desire the best gifts," 12:31) that immediately gets reframed — the very next line promises "a most excellent way," setting up chapter 13's redefinition of what actually matters most.

## Questions for study

1. Which gift or role in your own community have you privately ranked as more or less important?
2. Where do you feel like the disqualified foot, and where might you be the dismissive eye?
3. What would genuinely rejoicing at someone else's honor cost you this week?
