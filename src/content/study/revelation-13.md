One of the beast's heads looks fatally wounded, and then it isn't — and the reaction to the recovery is the detail worth stopping on: "the whole earth marveled at the beast" and asked "who is like the beast? Who is able to make war with him?" (13:3-4). A healed wound becomes proof of invincibility. Nobody in the scene asks how it happened. They just start worshipping.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 13`

## A second beast selling the first

The chapter doesn't stop at one monster. A second beast rises "like a lamb" but speaks like the dragon (13:11) — its whole job is marketing the first beast, performing signs, and building an image people are pressured to worship or be killed for refusing. Deception here isn't loud. It looks gentle right up until it doesn't.

## Bought and sold by a mark

No one may buy or sell without a mark on hand or forehead (13:16-17). The chapter ties worship directly to commerce — control the mark, and you control whether people eat. It's a chillingly practical mechanism for enforcing allegiance: not persuasion, just access.

## A number to calculate

> *"Here is wisdom. He who has understanding, let him calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of a man. His number is six hundred sixty-six."* — Revelation 13:18

The chapter ends on an invitation to think, not just to fear — "let him calculate" — as if understanding the beast is itself a form of resistance.

## Questions for study

1. Why does a healed wound convince the world of the beast's power rather than raise questions?
2. What makes the second beast's "gentle" deception more dangerous than open force?
3. Where does allegiance get quietly enforced through access to buying and selling in your own world?
