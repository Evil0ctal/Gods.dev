Paul has just spent three chapters arguing that Christ holds the entire universe together, seated above every throne and power. Then he closes the letter by naming ten people one by one — Tychicus, Onesimus, Aristarchus, Mark, Justus, Epaphras, Luke, Demas, Nympha, Archippus — like a foreman calling roll. The shift isn't a letdown. It's the letter's proof of concept: a cosmic Christ produces a very local, very named church.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible colossians 4`

## An open door, a careful tongue

Paul asks for prayer not for comfort but for opportunity — "that God may open to us a door for the word" (4:3) — and pairs it with instructions on how to talk to outsiders: walk in wisdom, redeem the time, keep your speech "seasoned with salt" (4:6). Salt preserves, and it stings a little too. Both are intended.

## The list is the theology

Onesimus gets one line here — "one of you," a "faithful and beloved brother" — and an entire other letter behind that line. He's a runaway slave being sent home with Paul's blessing, walking proof that the doctrine of chapters 1-2 changes households, not just minds. Nympha hosts a church in her own house. Archippus gets a public nudge: "take heed to the ministry... that you fulfill it" (4:17). Even a benediction, in this letter, comes with a job description attached.

> *"Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, that you may know how to answer each one."* — Colossians 4:6

## Questions for study

1. Who in your circle is, right now, the "open door" Paul prays for — a live opportunity you haven't walked through?
2. What would speech "seasoned with salt" sound like in a conversation you've been avoiding?
3. Read Onesimus's one line here alongside what you know of the letter to Philemon. What does his story add to the theology of chapters 1-2?
4. Archippus needed a public reminder to finish his ministry. What's your equivalent — the task named but not yet done?
