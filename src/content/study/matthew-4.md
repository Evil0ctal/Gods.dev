Right after the Spirit descends on Jesus at his baptism, that same Spirit leads him into the wilderness to be tempted (4:1). Matthew doesn't treat this as an unfortunate detour — it's the next necessary scene. Sonship confirmed by a voice from heaven gets tested immediately by hunger, height, and power. Identity is never left untested for long in this Gospel.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible matthew 4`

## Three temptations, one shape

Each temptation offers Jesus a shortcut to something legitimate: bread when he's genuinely hungry, proof of protection when trust is genuinely hard, kingdoms when he genuinely came to reign over the earth. The devil isn't offering evil disguised as good — he's offering good things detached from God's timing and method. Jesus answers each time with Scripture, not improvisation, quoting Deuteronomy back at the tempter.

> *"Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds out of the mouth of God."* — Matthew 4:4

## From desert to shoreline

The chapter pivots hard from isolation to public life. Jesus hears of John's arrest, withdraws to Galilee, and begins preaching the same message John preached: "Repent, for the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand" (4:17). Then, walking by the sea, he calls Peter, Andrew, James, and John away from actual nets with actual fish. Matthew notes they left "immediately" (4:20, 4:22) — no negotiation, no notice period.

> *"Come after me, and I will make you fishers for men."* — Matthew 4:19

## Questions for study

1. Which of the three temptations — bread, spectacle, or power — do you find most personally recognizable?
2. Jesus answers temptation with memorized Scripture. What role does that play, or fail to play, in your own life?
3. The disciples left immediately, mid-task. What would "immediately" cost you if Jesus called today?
