Jesus opens chapter 12 with a story clearly aimed at the men listening: a landowner leases out a vineyard and sends servant after servant to collect the harvest, only to have them beaten, killed, or turned away — until he sends his son, reasoning "they will respect my son" (12:6), and the tenants kill him too, thinking the inheritance will then be theirs. Mark says the religious leaders "knew that he spoke the parable against them" (12:12) — no ambiguity left by the end.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible mark 12`

## Traps, and how he avoids them

What follows is a series of gotcha questions engineered to force a costly answer: taxes to Caesar, resurrection logic from the Sadducees, which commandment is greatest. Each time, Jesus answers in a way that dissolves the trap rather than picking a side of it:

> *"Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's."* — Mark 12:17

## The whole law in two sentences

Asked for the greatest commandment, Jesus doesn't hedge or rank a top ten — he names two and says everything else hangs on them: love God completely, love your neighbor as yourself (12:29-31). It's the shortest possible summary of an enormous religious tradition, and the hardest to actually practice.

## A gift too small to notice, except he notices

The chapter closes on a widow dropping two small coins into the temple treasury, next to donors giving impressively large sums. Jesus's verdict inverts the visible math entirely — she gave more, because she gave everything she had, not merely what she could spare.

## Questions for study

1. The tenants think killing the heir gets them the inheritance. What faulty logic have you seen — or held — that resembles that?
2. "Render to Caesar... render to God" — where in your life do those two obligations actually compete?
3. Love God, love neighbor: which of the two do you neglect more in ordinary weeks?
4. The widow's gift was small in size, total in cost. What would giving "out of your poverty" rather than your abundance look like for you?
