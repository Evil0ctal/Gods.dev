Six days after Peter first calls him the Christ, Jesus takes three disciples up a mountain and, for a moment, stops hiding what he is. "His face shone like the sun, and his garments became as white as the light" (17:2). Moses and Elijah show up. Peter, predictably, wants to build something to commemorate it. The point of the scene is not the special effects — it is that this is who Jesus has been all along, underneath the ordinary face the disciples had gotten used to.

## A voice that ends the conversation

Peter is still talking about tents when a cloud interrupts him: *"This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Listen to him"* (17:5). Not "worship him" or "admire him" — listen to him. The instruction lands right after Jesus has been predicting his own death and the disciples have been arguing with that idea. The glory on the mountain doesn't replace the hard teaching; it backs it.

## The valley they couldn't fix

They come down to a father whose son the disciples couldn't heal (17:14-20). Jesus' diagnosis isn't lack of technique — it's a faith the size of nothing trying to move something enormous. *"If you have faith as a grain of mustard seed, you will tell this mountain, 'Move from here to there,' and it will move"* (17:20). It's a strange comfort: the problem was never the size of their faith relative to the mountain, but whether they had any at all.

## A coin in a fish's mouth

The chapter closes with a small, almost comic miracle — temple tax pulled from a fish's mouth (17:24-27) — so Jesus won't cause needless offense over a matter he could, by rights, ignore entirely. Even his exemptions come with restraint.

## Questions for study

1. What has become "ordinary" about Jesus in your own mind that the Transfiguration might correct?
2. "Listen to him" follows hard teaching about suffering. What hard teaching are you tempted to talk around instead of listening to?
3. Where in your life do you have a mountain and no visible mustard seed?
4. Why pay a tax he doesn't strictly owe? What does that restraint teach about picking battles?
