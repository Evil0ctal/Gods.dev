This chapter runs two harvest images back to back, and the contrast is the whole point. First, 144,000 stand with the Lamb on Mount Zion singing a song "no one could learn" except them (14:3) — a harvest gathered gently, called firstfruits. Then, near the chapter's end, a sickle reaps grapes "fully ripe" and throws them into a winepress trodden outside the city (14:18-20). Same image, opposite temperature.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 14`

## Three angels, one warning

Before either harvest, three angels fly through midheaven delivering an escalating message: fear God and give him glory (14:6-7); Babylon has fallen (14:8); don't take the mark, because the consequence is real and unmixed (14:9-10). The warnings come before the harvest, not after — there's still time to hear them while they're being spoken.

## Rest that follows work

> *"Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on... that they may rest from their labors, for their works follow with them."* — Revelation 14:13

This is one of the chapter's few tender lines, tucked between two warnings. Rest isn't presented as escape from having worked; the works come along, remembered rather than erased.

## Questions for study

1. Why place the firstfruits' song right next to the winepress harvest instead of separating them?
2. What is the effect of hearing all three angelic warnings before, not after, the judgment falls?
3. How does 14:13 change what "rest" means for the tired parts of your own life?
