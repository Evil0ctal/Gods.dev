After a chapter dismantling factional pride, Paul turns the same scrutiny on himself. He admits he didn't arrive in Corinth as a polished performer: "I was with you in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling" (2:3). For a city that prized rhetorical skill, that's a strange confession for a founding pastor to put in writing.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1corinthians 2`

## A deliberate weakness

His speech and preaching, he says, were "not in persuasive words of human wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power" (2:4). This wasn't false modesty — it was strategy. Paul didn't want their faith resting on how good he was at talking, "that your faith wouldn't stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God" (2:5). A gospel that depends on the messenger's charisma is fragile in exactly the way this one refuses to be.

## Wisdom hidden, now revealed

Paul does claim wisdom — just not the kind Corinth was ranking people by. It's "God's wisdom in a mystery, the wisdom that has been hidden" (2:7), unknown to the rulers of this world, who crucified "the Lord of glory" without recognizing him (2:8). The cross wasn't a failure of divine wisdom; it was divine wisdom operating so far outside the expected categories that the people running the world missed it entirely.

## The mind of Christ

> *"For who has known the mind of the Lord, that he should instruct him?" But we have Christ's mind.* — 1 Corinthians 2:16

## Questions for study

1. Where in your own life or ministry are you tempted to lean on polish instead of substance?
2. What would it cost you to present something important "in weakness" rather than dressed up?
3. How does "we have Christ's mind" sit with you — as comfort, responsibility, or both?
