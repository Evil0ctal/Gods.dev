Running out of wine at a wedding isn't a tragedy by any ordinary measure, but in a small honor-bound community it's a real embarrassment for the host family. Jesus's mother brings it to him, he initially resists, and then quietly has servants fill six stone jars, used for ceremonial washing rather than storage, with water that becomes the best wine of the night. John calls it the first of his signs, and the word matters: not just a miracle, but a signpost toward something.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible john 2`

## A quiet first sign
Nobody at the wedding except the servants even knows what happened.

> *"This beginning of his signs Jesus did in Cana of Galilee, and revealed his glory."* — John 2:11

## A loud second one
Contrast that with the temple scene right after: tables overturned, a whip of cords, animals driven out. This is public, confrontational, and impossible to miss. When asked for a sign to justify it, Jesus answers cryptically about the temple's destruction and rebuilding in three days, a saying nobody, including his own disciples, understands until much later.

> *"Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up."* — John 2:19

## Signs and what they signify
Both scenes are about the same thing: ordinary religious structures, a wedding's wine, a temple's sacrifices, being quietly replaced by something Jesus himself provides.

## Questions for study
1. Why does John call the wedding at Cana "the first of his signs" rather than just calling it a miracle?
2. Why might Jesus's action in the temple be so much more public than the wedding sign?
3. What does the "three days" saying mean, and why does John say the disciples only understood it later?
4. What ordinary structure in your own life might Jesus be quietly replacing?
