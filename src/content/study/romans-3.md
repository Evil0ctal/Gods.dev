Two chapters of relentless diagnosis land on a verdict with no exceptions:
"as it is written, 'There is no one righteous; no, not one'" (3:10). Paul
stacks quotation on quotation from the Psalms and Isaiah until the case
is closed — "that every mouth may be closed, and all the world may be
brought under the judgment of God" (3:19). It is, by design, the bleakest
point in the letter.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible romans 3`

## The verdict before the verse that changes everything

Verse 20 seals the case against self-improvement as a path to standing
with God: "by the works of the law, no flesh will be justified in his
sight." The law's job was never to make people righteous; it was to make
the diagnosis unmistakable — "through the law comes the knowledge of
sin."

## "But now"

Then comes one of the great pivots in scripture: "But now apart from the
law, a righteousness of God has been revealed" (3:21). Everything before
this verse was the problem stated in full. Everything after is the
solution, and Paul is careful to say it isn't new — the law and the
prophets already testified to it. It just wasn't achieved by keeping
rules. It's a righteousness given, "through faith in Jesus Christ, to all
and on all those who believe."

> *"Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in
> Christ Jesus."* — Romans 3:24

## No boasting left standing

Paul draws the obvious conclusion: if righteousness comes as a gift
received by faith, boasting is excluded "by a law of faith" (3:27). The
Jew and the Gentile end up in the identical position — justified the
same way, by the same faith, so that God is "the God of the Jews only...
and of Gentiles also" (3:29). A gift, by its nature, levels the room
that earns and merits could never level.

## Questions for study

1. Why might Paul need two full chapters of bad news before he can say
   "but now"?
2. If righteousness is a gift "apart from the law," what's left for the
   law to actually do?
3. "Boasting is excluded" — what would change in your daily posture if
   you took that literally?
4. Where do you still quietly believe your standing depends on your
   performance, despite what this chapter says?
