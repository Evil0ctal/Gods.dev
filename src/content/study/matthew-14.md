The chapter opens with a flashback to John the Baptist's execution — a grim, almost novelistic account of a drunken promise, a dancing girl, and a head delivered on a platter to satisfy a king's pride (14:1-12). Then Matthew cuts immediately to Jesus, hearing the news, withdrawing by boat to be alone. Grief gets no privacy, though: the crowds find him anyway, and Matthew says simply that Jesus "had compassion on them" and healed their sick (14:14). His own mourning doesn't close him off from other people's need.

## Five loaves, twelve baskets left over

When evening comes and the disciples want to send the crowd away to buy food, Jesus tells them, "You give them something to eat" (14:16). Five loaves and two fish feed a crowd Matthew numbers at five thousand men, not counting women and children — and twelve baskets of fragments are gathered afterward, one for each disciple who doubted there'd be enough. The leftovers aren't incidental; they're proof the shortage was never real to begin with.

## Sinking in plain sight of the answer

Later that night, Jesus walks toward the disciples' boat across the water, and Peter — alone among the twelve — asks to come to him on the water and does, briefly, before the wind distracts him and he begins to sink. Jesus catches him immediately.

> *"You of little faith, why did you doubt?"* — Matthew 14:31

Peter's failure isn't that he tried; it's that he looked away from the one reason the attempt was working at all.

## Questions for study

1. Jesus responds to grief by serving others rather than withdrawing fully. Where do you tend to do the opposite?
2. The disciples saw the shortage; Jesus saw what he was about to do with it. Where are you counting loaves instead of trusting the one holding them?
3. Peter's sinking began with a shift in attention, not a loss of ability. What currently distracts your attention from Jesus mid-step?
