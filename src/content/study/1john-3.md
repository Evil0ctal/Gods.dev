John opens with something close to astonishment — "what manner of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God" — and then immediately admits the label runs ahead of the reality: "it is not yet revealed what we will be" (3:1-2). He's comfortable holding an identity that's true now and still incomplete, named in advance of what it will fully look like.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1john 3`

## Sin as a category, not a slip

Verses 4-9 draw a sharper line than many readers expect — whoever practices sin is of the devil, and whoever is born of God cannot go on sinning. John isn't describing perfection after a single lapse; he's describing a settled pattern of life, a direction someone is actually walking in, distinguishing an occasional stumble from a life defined by ongoing rebellion.

## Cain as the negative example

John reaches back to Cain, who killed his brother because his own works were evil and his brother's righteous (3:12), to make the sibling-love command concrete and ancient rather than a new sentimental idea. The test of love keeps landing in practical terms — not just feeling but sharing what you have with a brother in need (3:17).

> *"Let's not love in word only, neither with the tongue only, but in deed and truth."* — 1 John 3:18

## Questions for study

1. How does it feel to hold, as John does in 3:1-2, an identity ("children of God") that's true now but not yet fully shown?
2. What's the difference John draws between an occasional sin and "practicing" sin as a pattern of life (3:4-9)?
3. Verse 17 makes love concrete through material sharing — where is there a specific, practical need in front of you right now?
