A centurion — an occupying officer, no less — sends Jewish elders to ask Jesus to heal his servant, then sends a second message before Jesus even arrives: don't trouble yourself, just say the word. He compares it to his own chain of command; he gives an order and it happens. Jesus, who is rarely reported to be surprised by anything, is surprised by this. "I have not found such great faith, no, not in Israel."

## A funeral interrupted

At Nain, Jesus meets a funeral procession for a widow's only son and, without being asked, stops it. "Young man, I tell you, arise." Luke notes what Jesus feels first: compassion for the mother, before any word is spoken to the dead.

## Are you the one?

John the Baptist, now in prison, sends disciples to ask whether Jesus is really the one they've been waiting for — doubt from the very man who once pointed at him. Jesus doesn't rebuke the question; he answers with evidence: the blind see, the lame walk, the poor hear good news.

## Much forgiven, much loved

At a Pharisee's dinner, a woman known for her sin washes Jesus's feet with tears and hair, uninvited and unashamed. Jesus tells his host that her extravagant love is proportional to how much she knows she's been forgiven — and asks whether Simon, who owes so much less by his own reckoning, has managed to love at all.

> *"I have not found such great faith, no, not in Israel."* — Luke 7:9

> *"Her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much."* — Luke 7:47

## Questions for study

1. What does the centurion understand about authority that leads him to such confidence?
2. Why doesn't Jesus criticize John for doubting from prison?
3. What does the woman's extravagance reveal that the Pharisee's correctness doesn't?
4. Where do you find it harder to believe: that Jesus can heal from a distance, or that he forgives someone as messy as you?
