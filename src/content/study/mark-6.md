Nazareth knew Jesus too well to believe in him. "Is this not the carpenter, the son of Mary...?" (6:3) is less hostile than baffled — how does the kid we watched grow up turn into this? Mark records the result flatly: "he could do no mighty work there... and he marveled because of their unbelief" (6:5-6). It is one of the few places in the Gospels where Jesus himself is described as surprised.

## Sent out with nothing

From that scene of unbelief, Jesus sends the twelve out with almost nothing — no bread, no bag, no money, a staff only (6:8-9) — to do exactly what he's just been rejected for doing. Traveling light isn't minimalism for its own sake; it puts the message, not the messengers' resources, in the position of carrying the weight.

## A birthday that ends on a platter

The chapter's dark center is Herod's banquet: a rash oath, a dancer's request engineered by her mother, and John the Baptist's head delivered because a king was too proud to break a public promise (6:21-28). Mark places it deliberately between the sending of the twelve and their return — a reminder of exactly what this mission runs up against.

## Loaves, water, and panic in the boat

Then, in the same chapter: five loaves and two fish feed a crowd Mark numbers at five thousand men, with twelve baskets left over (6:41-44) — and Jesus walking across a lake to his disciples in a fourth-watch storm.

> *"He immediately spoke with them, and said to them, 'Cheer up! It is I! Don't be afraid.'"* — Mark 6:50

## Questions for study

1. Where has familiarity with someone — or with Jesus — made it harder for you to see them clearly?
2. The twelve went out with nothing. What provisions do you lean on that might need setting down?
3. Herod's tragedy starts with a promise made to look good in front of guests. Where do you feel that same pressure?
4. "It is I. Don't be afraid" — what storm would you want to hear that spoken into right now?
