Zacchaeus does not walk up to Jesus like a normal adult. He is rich, he is short, and he is despised as a collaborator, so he runs ahead of the crowd and climbs a sycamore tree like a boy trying to see a parade. Luke likes this kind of detail — the posture tells you more than a paragraph of description could. Jesus stops under the tree, invites himself to dinner, and by the end of the meal Zacchaeus is giving away half his fortune. Conversion, in Luke, keeps showing up in the wallet.

## A tree and a table
The crowd grumbles that Jesus has gone to be the guest of "a sinner." Jesus does not argue the label. He just says why he came.

> *"For the Son of Man came to seek and to save that which was lost."* — Luke 19:10

## Money put to work
The parable of the minas follows immediately, and it is easy to flatten into a simple lesson about effort. But notice what the third servant actually says: he was afraid of a hard master, so he did nothing. Fear, not laziness exactly, is what buries the coin.

## A king who weeps
Then the tone shifts hard. The crowd shouts "Hosanna," the Pharisees complain, and Jesus answers with the line about stones crying out — and then, looking at the city, he weeps over it. A king entering in triumph, crying, because the people cheering him do not know what they are cheering for.

## Questions for study
1. What does Zacchaeus's response to Jesus cost him, specifically?
2. What does the third servant's excuse reveal about how he sees his master?
3. Why does Jesus weep over Jerusalem right after the crowd's celebration?
4. Where in your own life is fear doing the work that trust should be doing?
