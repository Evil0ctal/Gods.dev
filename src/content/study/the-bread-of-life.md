The crowd that tracks Jesus down across the lake has an obvious motive — he just fed five thousand of them with a boy's lunch, and free bread has a way of generating repeat customers. Jesus names it outright: "you seek me, not because you saw signs, but because you ate of the loaves" (6:26). What follows is not a second miracle. It's a teaching that gets progressively harder to swallow.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible john 6`

## From bread to body

Jesus reframes the request for another meal into a claim about himself: *"I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will not be hungry"* (6:35). Then he pushes further, into language his listeners find genuinely offensive — eating his flesh, drinking his blood. This isn't Jesus softening his message for a crowd that's already invested in him. It's the opposite.

## The math of who stays

By the end of the discourse, John reports the cost plainly: many of the disciples say, *"This is a hard saying! Who can listen to it?"* (6:60), and many "went back, and walked no more with him" (6:66). The chapter that opens with a crowd of thousands closes by asking the twelve if they, too, want to leave.

> *"Simon Peter answered him, 'Lord, to whom would we go? You have the words of eternal life.'"* — John 6:68

## Questions for study

1. Why does Jesus seem to make his teaching harder rather than easier as the crowd's interest grows?
2. What's the difference between following Jesus for what he provides and following him for who he is?
3. What do you make of Peter's answer in 6:68? Is it confidence, resignation, or both?
4. Where in your own life have you stayed with a "hard saying" rather than walking away?
