After the third bowl turns rivers to blood, the chapter pauses to let someone explain why: the angel of the waters says it's deserved, "because they poured out the blood of the saints and the prophets, and you have given them blood to drink. They deserve this" (16:5-6). Most plagues in Revelation just happen. This one comes with a stated rationale, spoken by the very element being judged.

## Refusing to repent under fire

Bowl after bowl lands — sores, blood, scorching sun, darkness — and each time the response is the same: people "blasphemed the name of God" and "didn't repent" (16:9, 11). The plagues aren't producing change. They're revealing what was already fixed.

## A word that became a place

The sixth bowl dries up the Euphrates and gathers kings for war at a place named Harmagedon (16:16) — a word that has since outgrown this single verse to become shorthand for any final catastrophe. In context, it's simply where deceived armies get assembled for a battle they don't know they've already lost.

## "It is done"

> *"A great voice came out of the temple of heaven, from the throne, saying, 'It is done!'"* — Revelation 16:17

The seventh bowl doesn't describe more suffering first — it announces completion, and the earthquake and hail that follow are aftermath, not buildup. The sentence has already been passed before the details arrive.

## Questions for study

1. Why might the angel of the waters explain the third bowl's judgment rather than let it stand unexplained?
2. What does the refusal to repent under bowl after bowl suggest about the purpose of the plagues?
3. What do you make of "it is done" being declared before the final destructive details are even listed?
