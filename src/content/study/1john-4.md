John opens with an instruction easy to miss the weight of — "don't believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God" (4:1) — because false prophets had already gone out into the world. The test he gives isn't elaborate discernment techniques; it's a single confession, whether a spirit acknowledges Jesus Christ come in the flesh. Doctrine, for John, is never a side issue from love — it's the ground the love has to stand on.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1john 4`

## Not that we loved God

Verses 9-10 make the sequence explicit and deliberately reverses the expected order: love isn't proven by our reaching toward God but by God sending his Son as the atoning sacrifice while we hadn't reached at all. Every command to love each other that follows in the chapter rests on this — we're not generating love from nothing, we're passing along something we first received.

## Perfect love casting out fear

The chapter's most quoted line — "perfect love casts out fear, because fear has punishment" (4:18) — isn't a general encouragement against anxiety. In context, it's specifically about confidence on judgment day, boldness rather than dread, because love that has matured leaves no room for the fear of punishment to coexist with it.

> *"Whoever doesn't love doesn't know God, for God is love."* — 1 John 4:8

## Questions for study

1. Why does John insist on testing spirits by a specific confession (4:2-3) rather than trusting sincerity or spiritual experience alone?
2. How does reversing the order in verses 9-10 — God loved first — change how you think about your own capacity to love others?
3. Where does fear still have a foothold in your relationship with God, and what would "perfected" love (4:18) look like there instead?
