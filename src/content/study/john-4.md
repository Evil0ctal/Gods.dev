Jesus is "wearied from his journey" when he sits down at Jacob's well around noon, a detail John doesn't have to include, and does anyway. A woman comes to draw water at an hour most people would avoid the heat, which likely tells you something about wanting to avoid other people too. Jesus asks her for a drink, crossing two social lines at once — she's a Samaritan, and she's a woman alone talking to a Jewish man in public.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible john 4`

## Thirsty, and offering water anyway
Jesus, who just asked her for a drink, pivots to offering her something else entirely.

> *"Whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst again."* — John 4:14

## Worship without a zip code
When she raises the old argument about the right mountain to worship on, Samaritan or Jewish, Jesus reframes the whole question.

> *"God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth."* — John 4:24

## A word that travels
The chapter closes with a second story: an official begs Jesus to come heal his dying son in person. Jesus doesn't go. He just says the boy will live, and the man believes the word alone and heads home, arriving to find his son healed at the exact hour Jesus spoke.

## Questions for study
1. Why does John bother to mention that Jesus was tired and thirsty before this conversation?
2. What does Jesus's answer about "spirit and truth" do to the geography argument the woman raises?
3. Why might the official's story be placed right after the Samaritan woman's?
4. What would it mean to trust a word from Jesus without needing to see the result first?
