The women go to the tomb early, carrying spices, planning to finish a burial — which tells you they expected exactly what everyone expects after a death like that. What they find instead isn't a resolution to their errand; it's a question that makes the errand nonsensical.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible luke 24`

## The wrong question at the right tomb
Two messengers meet the women's grief with a question that reframes everything.

> *"Why do you seek the living among the dead? He isn't here, but is risen."* — Luke 24:5-6

The apostles, told this, dismiss it as "idle talk." Even the people closest to Jesus needed more than a report to believe it.

## Recognized in the breaking of bread
Two disciples walk to Emmaus, unpack the whole tragedy to a stranger who joins them, and don't recognize him until he does something they'd seen him do before — takes bread, gives thanks, breaks it. Recognition comes through a gesture, not an argument.

> *"Weren't our hearts burning within us, while he spoke to us along the way?"* — Luke 24:32

## Sent, not just reassured
Jesus doesn't appear to the disciples just to prove he's alive. He opens their understanding of Scripture, tells them what's to come, and sends them out as witnesses — which is exactly how Luke's next book, Acts, picks up the story.

## Questions for study
1. Why does the news of the resurrection first sound like "idle talk" even to Jesus's own followers?
2. What does it mean that the Emmaus disciples recognize Jesus specifically in the breaking of bread?
3. How does this chapter set up the beginning of Acts?
4. Where might you be looking for "the living" in a place that's already empty?
