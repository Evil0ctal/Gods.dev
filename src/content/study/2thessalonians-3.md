After two chapters about cosmic reversal and coming judgment, this final chapter deals with something almost comically small: some in the church had quit working, living off others, apparently on the theory that if the end was near, ordinary labor was beneath the moment. Paul disagrees, sharply.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 2thessalonians 3`

## Idle, not resting

He names it directly: some "walk disorderly, not working at all, but are busybodies" (3:11) — idle in a way that curdles into meddling. Free time, unoccupied by work, apparently found something to do anyway: other people's business.

## Paul's own receipts

His rebuttal leans on his own record rather than a bare command: "we didn't eat bread from anyone's hand for nothing, but in labor and travail worked night and day" (3:8), specifically so as not to be a burden, even though he had every right to expect support. He isn't asking anything of them he didn't already do himself.

## A rule with no exceptions offered

The line that follows is famous for its bluntness: "If anyone will not work, neither let him eat" (3:10) — not a comment on those unable to work, but on those unwilling. And immediately after the hard line, a gentler one: "do not grow weary in doing good" (3:13), aimed at everyone else who might get tired of carrying the idle.

> *"If anyone will not work, neither let him eat."* — 2 Thessalonians 3:10

## Questions for study

1. Where might urgency about "bigger things" be giving you cover to neglect an ordinary responsibility?
2. Paul backs his hard instruction with his own example rather than just authority. Where does your own credibility need that kind of receipt?
3. "Busybodies" suggests idle time filled by meddling rather than rest. Does that describe any of your own unoccupied hours?
4. Who around you might be growing weary in doing good, and what would it cost you to lighten that load?
