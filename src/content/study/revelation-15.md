The people singing in this chapter are standing on "a sea of glass mixed with fire" (15:2) — an image that shouldn't work, glass and fire together, calm and danger in the same substance. They're the ones who came out victorious over the beast, and their position is the point: they're standing on top of the very thing that should have swallowed them.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 15`

## A song for the last plagues, not despite them

They sing the song of Moses and the song of the Lamb before the seven last plagues are even released — worship placed deliberately ahead of the worst chapter still coming, not after it's survived.

> *"Great and marvelous are your works, Lord God, the Almighty! Righteous and true are your ways, you King of the nations."* — Revelation 15:3

## A temple no one can enter

Once the seven angels come out with the seven plagues, the temple fills with smoke from God's glory, and "no one was able to enter into the temple, until the seven plagues of the seven angels would be finished" (15:8). Even access to God's presence pauses while judgment runs its course — a strange, sobering detail in a chapter otherwise full of praise.

## A short chapter doing a lot of holding

Fifteen chapters in, this one is brief — barely more than a scene change — but it holds the position between plague and plague, singing and sealing, victory already won and consequences not yet finished.

## Questions for study

1. What does it mean to sing "great and marvelous are your works" before the worst plagues arrive rather than after?
2. Why might even access to God's presence pause during a period of judgment?
3. Where in your life have you had to worship before the outcome, not after it?
