John doesn't flinch from an awkward detail: Jesus's own brothers tell him to go make a public show of himself in Jerusalem, and the narrator adds flatly that "even his brothers didn't believe in him." Family proximity, it turns out, guarantees nothing about recognition. Jesus goes up to the feast anyway, but quietly, and teaches in the temple in the middle of the festival.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible john 7`

## A crowd that can't agree
Opinion about Jesus splits every direction, some call him good, some call him a deceiver, some wonder if the authorities secretly know he's the Christ and are just letting it slide.

## A river out of the dry season
On the last, biggest day of the festival, Jesus stands up and makes a public claim in front of everyone.

> *"If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink!"* — John 7:37

The Feast of Tabernacles had water-pouring rituals built into it; Jesus's invitation lands right on top of that imagery, offering himself as the source instead of the ritual.

## Officers who came back empty-handed
The chief priests send temple police to arrest him. They come back without him, and when asked why, their excuse is disarmingly simple.

> *"No man ever spoke like this man!"* — John 7:46

## Questions for study
1. What does it mean that Jesus's own brothers didn't believe in him at this point?
2. Why does the crowd's opinion of Jesus split so many different ways?
3. How does Jesus's invitation to "come and drink" connect to the festival happening around him?
4. What kept the officers from making the arrest they were sent to make?
