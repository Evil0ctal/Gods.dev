Ten bridesmaids wait for a groom who's running late, and five of them run out of oil for their lamps at the worst possible moment (25:1-13). What stops the story from being a simple readiness lesson is verse 9: the five who have oil refuse to share it — *"not so, lest there be not enough for us and you"* — because some things simply cannot be lent at the last minute. Faith, apparently, is one of them.

## The excuse that damns a servant

The parable of the talents (25:14-30) gives three servants unequal amounts of money and judges them not on the amount but on what they did with it. The servant who buried his one talent defends himself by calling the master "a hard man" — and it's precisely that fearful, defensive picture of the master that produces the paralysis. Fear of a harsh master, not lack of resources, is what buries the talent.

## The judgment where nobody saw it coming

The chapter's final scene — sheep separated from goats — turns on a single repeated line: *"Most certainly I tell you, inasmuch as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me"* (25:40). What's striking is that both groups are surprised. Neither the sheep nor the goats remember serving or ignoring Christ directly — they only remember the hungry, the stranger, the prisoner, ordinary and unglamorous.

## Questions for study

1. What "oil" in your life — character, habits, faith itself — can't be borrowed from someone else at the last minute?
2. What picture of God is currently justifying any caution or paralysis in your own life, like the third servant's excuse?
3. Both sheep and goats are surprised by the verdict. What does that say about where you should be looking for Christ right now?
4. Name one "least of these" you have genuine, regular access to this week.
