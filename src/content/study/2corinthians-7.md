This chapter opens mid-relief. Paul had sent a hard letter to Corinth,
then waited — anxious enough that he says even in Macedonia "our flesh
had no relief, but we were afflicted on every side" (7:5). Then Titus
arrives with good news, and the whole tone of the letter lifts. You can
feel a man exhale in real time.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 2corinthians 7`

## The comfort of showing up

Verse 6 is almost throwaway but worth pausing on: "God, who comforts the
lowly, comforted us by the coming of Titus." Not by a vision, not by an
argument — by a friend arriving with a report. Paul's theology of comfort
is thoroughly unglamorous: sometimes God's consolation has a face and a
travel itinerary.

## The sorrow that heals

*"Godly sorrow works repentance to salvation, which brings no regret,
but the sorrow of the world works death."* — 2 Corinthians 7:10

This is the chapter's sharpest tool, and it's a genuine distinction, not
a platitude. Worldly sorrow spirals — shame, self-pity, paralysis, more
shame. Godly sorrow moves: it produces "earnestness," "clearing of
yourselves," "indignation," "concern" (7:11) — a whole cluster of verbs,
all pointed toward repair rather than collapse. One kind of sorrow buries
you. The other digs you out.

## Confidence restored

Paul closes almost giddy: "I rejoice that in everything I am confident
concerning you" (7:16). After a painful letter and an anxious wait, that
confidence is not naive optimism — it's relief earned by the Corinthians'
actual response.

## Questions for study

1. Think of a recent regret. Did it move you toward repair (godly
   sorrow) or toward paralysis (worldly sorrow)? What told the
   difference?
2. Who has been "a Titus" to you — comfort that arrived as a person, not
   an idea?
3. What would it take to write the hard letter you've been avoiding,
   trusting the relationship enough to risk it?
