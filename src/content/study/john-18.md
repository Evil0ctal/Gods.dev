Judas leads a detachment of soldiers to a garden Jesus has used often enough that his betrayer knows exactly where to find him (18:2). There's no ambush here, no manhunt — Jesus walks toward them and asks who they're looking for. When they say his name, he answers "I am he," and the arresting party falls backward to the ground (18:6). Even in custody, John wants you to notice who's actually in control of the scene.

## The sword and the cup

Peter draws a blade and takes off a man's ear. Jesus heals what Peter broke and puts the sword away himself: *"Put the sword into its sheath. The cup which the Father has given me, shall I not drink it?"* (18:11). Peter wanted to fight for a kingdom Jesus had already told him wasn't that kind of kingdom.

## Denial by firelight

While Jesus is questioned inside, Peter warms himself at a charcoal fire in the courtyard and denies knowing him — three times, the last one right as a rooster crows (18:15-27). The bravado of the garden and the fear at the fire belong to the same man within the same hour.

## Not of this world

Before Pilate, Jesus makes the claim that reframes everything: *"My Kingdom is not of this world... my Kingdom is not from here"* (18:36). Pilate's response — *"What is truth?"* (18:38) — is a question asked by a man standing in front of the answer and not recognizing it.

## Questions for study

1. Jesus tells Peter to put away the sword he drew to defend him. When is fighting for a good cause still the wrong instinct?
2. What do you make of the gap between Peter's courage in the garden and his fear at the fire?
3. "My Kingdom is not of this world" — what does that claim rule in, and what does it rule out?
4. Pilate asks "What is truth?" to Truth himself (14:6). What keeps people from recognizing an answer standing right in front of them?
