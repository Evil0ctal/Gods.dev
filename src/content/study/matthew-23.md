Somewhere in the middle of a long indictment of the scribes and Pharisees, Jesus lands on one of the sharpest images he ever used: *"You blind guides, who strain out a gnat, and swallow a camel!"* (23:24). Tithing the herbs from your garden down to the leaf while ignoring justice, mercy, and faith — meticulous about the miniature, oblivious to the massive. It's not a chapter about hypocrisy in general; it's a chapter about a very specific, very human trick of attention.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible matthew 23`

## Do as they say, not as they do

Jesus opens by telling the crowds to obey the scribes' teaching but not copy their example (23:2-3) — a distinction that assumes true teaching can outrun the character of the person delivering it. Then he lists what's actually driving the performance: wide phylacteries, long robes, best seats, the title "Rabbi" — all of it built for an audience, none of it for God.

## Seven times, "woe to you"

The sevenfold "woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites" (23:13-29) isn't a rant losing steam — each woe names a distinct failure: locking people out of the kingdom, converting people into something worse, majoring on trivia while neglecting justice, cleaning the outside of the cup while the inside stays filthy, whitewashing tombs full of decay.

## A lament, not a victory lap

The chapter that reads like fury ends in grief: *"Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets... how often I would have gathered your children together, even as a hen gathers her chickens under her wings, and you would not!"* (23:37). The harshest chapter in Matthew closes with Jesus wanting, and being refused.

## Questions for study

1. Where in your own life is there a "gnat" getting careful attention while a "camel" goes unnoticed?
2. What's the difference between correcting bad teaching and copying bad character, as 23:2-3 assumes you can do?
3. Which of the seven woes stings the most if you're honest about your own religious habits?
4. The chapter ends in longing, not contempt. How does that change how you read everything before it?
