Jesus spends most of Matthew 6 on three ordinary religious acts — giving, praying, fasting — and attacks the same failure mode in all three: doing them to be seen. He's blunt about the reward structure: those who give, pray, or fast for an audience "have received their reward" already, in full, and there's nothing left for God to add (6:2, 5, 16). The chapter is less about what to do and more about who you're doing it for.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible matthew 6`

## A prayer that reorients before it requests

At the center of the chapter sits the Lord's Prayer, and its structure is worth noticing: it opens with God's name, kingdom, and will before it ever gets to daily bread, debts, and temptation. Jesus is modeling a prayer life that starts by realigning the pray-er, not by filing a request list.

> *"Your kingdom come. Your will be done, as in heaven, so on earth."* — Matthew 6:10

## Treasure, eyes, and two masters

The second half pivots to possessions and anxiety. "Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also" (6:21) is not abstract financial advice — it's a diagnostic tool: follow the money and you'll find the actual object of worship. Jesus then names the option most people quietly try to avoid naming: "You cannot serve God and Mammon" (6:24). The chapter closes on birds, lilies, and the command not to be anxious about tomorrow, because today already has enough to deal with.

> *"Therefore don't be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself."* — Matthew 6:34

## Questions for study

1. Where in your own religious practice, if you're honest, is there an audience you're performing for?
2. If you traced your spending and calendar like Jesus traces treasure to the heart, what would it reveal you actually worship?
3. What specific tomorrow-shaped worry is Jesus' command in 6:34 speaking directly to right now?
