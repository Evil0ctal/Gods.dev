Paul spends sixteen verses on widows before he ever gets to elders — an unusual budget of attention for a letter this short, and it tells you something about what the church in Ephesus actually looked like: a body organized enough to run something resembling a welfare roll. Chapter 5 reads less like theology and more like bylaws.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1timothy 5`

## A list, not a sentiment

Verses 3-16 describe an actual enrollment: widows over sixty, married once, with a track record of hospitality and washing feet (5:9-10). Below that age, Paul's advice is blunt — remarry, run a household (5:14). That offends modern ears expecting "just help everyone," but the logic is resource, not resentment: family carries family first (5:4, 8), so the church's list can carry those who genuinely have no one else.

> *"Honor widows who are widows indeed."* — 1 Timothy 5:3

## Elders under the same rules they enforce

Verses 17-22 turn the camera on leadership. Elders who lead well get "double honor" — likely meaning pay — but they are not above accusation, only above *casual* accusation: two or three witnesses required (5:19), public correction when guilty (5:20), no favoritism (5:21). Authority and accountability travel together here; Paul won't let one exist without the other.

## The stomach and the wine

Then, oddly, a personal aside about Timothy's health (5:23) — wine for a bad stomach, wedged between commands about disciplining elders. It's a small, human seam in an administrative chapter, a reminder that this is a real letter to a real, tired person running a real church.

## Questions for study

1. What does Paul's insistence that families support their own widows (5:4, 8) protect against?
2. Why require multiple witnesses before an accusation against a leader (5:19) — what does that guard, and against whom?
3. Where in a group you belong to do "honor" and "accountability" need to travel together but currently don't?
4. Does verse 23 sitting where it does surprise you — a physical, personal detail in the middle of church policy?
