Ephesians 2 doesn't ease into its subject. It opens with a diagnosis: dead in trespasses and sins — not sick, not searching, dead (2:1). Before saying a word about grace, Paul insists on the size of the problem it solves.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible ephesians 2`

## Dead, not merely struggling

The language of verses 1-3 is corpse language, not patient language. Sick people can improve with effort; dead people need someone else to raise them. It's an unflattering starting point, and Paul means it to be — the size of the rescue depends on the size of the diagnosis.

## Two words carrying the whole chapter

> *"But God, being rich in mercy, for his great love with which he loved us... made us alive together with Christ."* — Ephesians 2:4-5

> *"For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, that no one would boast."* — Ephesians 2:8-9

The turn happens entirely outside the dead man's control. That's the whole point of the word "gift."

## A wall that came down

The second half widens the lens from individual rescue to merged humanity: Jew and Gentile, once divided by "the middle wall of partition" (2:14), remade into "one new man" and "fellow citizens" of a single household (2:19). Personal resurrection and social reconciliation are, for Paul, one event, not two.

## Questions for study

1. Why might "dead" be a more accurate diagnosis than "struggling" or "searching"?
2. What has grace already done in you that you had no part in producing?
3. What walls in your own setting does this chapter say are already down, whether or not people act like it?
