Asked a yes-or-no question — "Are these things so?" — Stephen answers with a speech that runs the length of the whole book of Genesis and Exodus in miniature: Abraham, Joseph, Moses, the wilderness, David, Solomon's temple. It's the longest speech in Acts, and it is not a defense in the ordinary sense. Stephen isn't trying to survive the room. He's trying to show his accusers the pattern they're currently repeating.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible acts 7`

## The pattern underneath the history

The recurring shape of Stephen's history lesson is Israel resisting the very deliverers God sends — rejecting Joseph, rejecting Moses, building a golden calf while Moses is still on the mountain. By the time he reaches the point, the indictment lands with force:

> *"You stiff-necked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit! As your fathers did, so you do."* — Acts 7:51

## A vision no one else sees

Cut to the heart, the council rushes him. Stephen, "full of the Holy Spirit," looks up and sees something no one around him can: Jesus standing at God's right hand (7:55-56) — not seated, as if he had risen to receive him.

## Two prayers, echoing one death

As the stones fall, Stephen prays twice, and both prayers echo Jesus from the cross: *"Lord Jesus, receive my spirit!"* and *"Lord, don't hold this sin against them!"* (7:59-60). Saul, watching the coats, is mentioned almost in passing — Luke lets the detail sit there, unexplained, waiting for the next chapter.

## Questions for study

1. Why answer a specific accusation with the entire sweep of Israelite history?
2. What's the effect of Stephen's vision of Jesus "standing" rather than seated?
3. What does it cost Stephen to pray forgiveness over the very men stoning him?
4. Where does Saul's silent presence in this chapter change how you read what comes next?
