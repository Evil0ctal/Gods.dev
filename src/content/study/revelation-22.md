The final chapter of Revelation closes not with a warning or a summary but with a word said three times by three different voices: "Come" (22:17). The Spirit says it, the bride says it, and whoever hears is told to say it too. A book this dense with judgment, plague, and collapse ends on an invitation that's still open when the last page turns.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible revelation 22`

## A river and a tree, undoing an old exile

The river of the water of life and the tree of life reappear here (22:1-2), unmistakably echoing the garden Genesis closed off. What was guarded and lost at the beginning of the story is restored and freely offered at its end — the whole Bible's arc completed in two verses.

## Don't worship the angel, again

For the second time in two chapters, John falls down to worship the angel showing him these things, and is corrected again: "Worship God" (22:9). Even at the very end of the vision, the temptation to worship the messenger instead of the message's source hasn't gone away.

## Come

> *"He who is thirsty, let him come. He who desires, let him take the water of life freely."* — Revelation 22:17

No qualification, no fee, no waiting period — just thirst as the only prerequisite, and an offer still standing.

## Questions for study

1. Why end the entire book on an open invitation rather than a final warning?
2. What does the return of the tree of life say about how the Bible's story resolves what Genesis started?
3. What does it mean that "thirsty" is the only qualification named in 22:17?
