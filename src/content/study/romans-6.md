Chapter 5 ended with a claim so generous it practically invites abuse:
where sin increased, grace increased even more. Paul knows exactly what
that sounds like, and he opens chapter 6 by naming the obvious
objection out loud: "Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound?"
His answer is as blunt as the question: "May it never be!" (6:1-2).

> Read it in the terminal: `bible romans 6`

## Baptized into a death

Paul's argument isn't a rule against sinning; it's a claim about identity.
Believers were "baptized into his death" and "buried with him through
baptism into death" so that, "just as Christ was raised from the dead
through the glory of the Father, so we also might walk in newness of
life" (6:3-4). The logic runs: you can't keep living in a house that's
been demolished. Sin's claim on you was tied to the old self, and that
self died.

> *"For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal
> life in Christ Jesus our Lord."* — Romans 6:23

## Consider it true, then act like it

Verse 11 gives the pivotal instruction: "consider yourselves also to be
dead to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus." Not "try harder" — Paul's
whole approach is that the change in status has to be *reckoned*, taken
as fact, before it can be lived out. He follows immediately with
practical commands: don't let sin reign, don't present your body's parts
to it as weapons, but "present yourselves to God" (6:12-13). Theology
first, then the imperative — never the reverse.

## Slaves either way

The chapter closes with an image that strips away any illusion of
neutral freedom: everyone is a slave to something, either sin (which
pays out in death) or obedience (which leads to righteousness) (6:16-18).
There's no unmastered middle ground on offer — only a choice of which
master, and wages that look nothing alike.

## Questions for study

1. Why does Paul answer "should we sin more?" with identity rather than
   with a list of rules?
2. What does it mean, practically, to "consider" yourself dead to sin
   before you feel any different?
3. Sin's wage is death; God's gift is life. Where in your life have you
   been quietly collecting the wrong wage?
4. Everyone serves some master, Paul says. What is currently vying for
   that position in your life besides God?
