The chapter opens with one of the boldest commands in the letter: imitate God. Not admire from a distance, not merely obey — imitate, the way a child picks up a parent's mannerisms without being told to (5:1).

> Read it in the terminal: `bible ephesians 5`

## Children of light

> *"You were once darkness, but are now light in the Lord."* — Ephesians 5:8

Not "you were in darkness" — you were darkness, and now you are light. Identity changed here, not just conduct, and the behavior Paul asks for in verses 3-14 follows from that change rather than earning it.

## Love that empties itself

> *"Walk in love, even as Christ also loved you, and gave himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God."* — Ephesians 5:2

That pattern — love that gives rather than takes — becomes the template a few verses later for marriage, a relationship Paul frames first as mutual submission (5:21) before he gets specific about husbands and wives. Whatever else the household instructions of 5:22-33 are doing, they're doing it under a heading of self-giving love, not authority for its own sake.

## An old line, reused

Paul quotes Genesis — the two becoming one flesh — and then says plainly he's really talking about Christ and the church (5:31-32). Marriage, in this chapter, is less the subject than the illustration.

## Questions for study

1. What would it look like this week to imitate God rather than just obey a rule?
2. Where do you still think of yourself as "in darkness" rather than as light, full stop?
3. How does reading marriage as an illustration of self-giving love change what the chapter is actually asking of you?
