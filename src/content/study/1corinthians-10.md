Paul opens by pointing out something the Corinthians hadn't connected: the wilderness generation had every spiritual advantage available — the cloud, the sea, manna, water from the rock — and most of them still didn't make it (10:1-5). Having the right experiences and sacraments, in other words, guarantees nothing. "These things happened as examples for us," he writes, treating Israel's failures not as ancient history but as a warning label addressed directly to a church that assumed its own privileges made it safe.

## Confidence is the risk, not the exception

> *"Therefore let him who thinks he stands be careful that he doesn't fall."* — 1 Corinthians 10:12

This verse sits right after a list of specific wilderness failures — idolatry, sexual immorality, testing God, grumbling. Paul's point isn't that failure is likely for everyone equally; it's that the people most at risk are the ones who've stopped considering it possible.

## An escape built into the test

> *"God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted above what you are able, but will with the temptation also make the way of escape."* — 1 Corinthians 10:13

Read alongside verse 12, this isn't a comforting platitude so much as a practical instruction: the way out exists, but you have to be looking for it rather than assuming you're above needing one.

## One table, not two

The chapter's back half returns to the meat-sacrificed-to-idols question from chapter 8, but sharpens it: participating at the Lord's table and participating in idol worship are both real participations, and you can't casually straddle both (10:14-22). The closing instruction — do everything to the glory of God (10:31) — is less a slogan than the only sustainable answer to a life full of ambiguous choices.

## Questions for study

1. Where has confidence in your own standing made you less careful, not more?
2. What "way of escape" have you been ignoring because acknowledging the temptation felt like admitting weakness?
3. Is there a place you're trying to belong at two tables that don't actually mix?
