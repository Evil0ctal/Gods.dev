Peter, who was himself once told by Jesus to "feed my sheep," turns that same charge over to the elders of these scattered churches — shepherd the flock, not under compulsion but willingly, not for shameful gain but eagerly, not lording it over people but being examples (5:2-3). Coming from someone who had personally received the charge from Christ, the instruction carries more weight than generic leadership advice.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible 1peter 5`

## A predator, not an abstraction

Verse 8's image of the devil as "a roaring lion, walking about, seeking whom he may devour" is deliberately physical and predatory, not a vague metaphor for temptation in general. Peter pairs the warning with a simple prescription — be sober, be watchful — treating vigilance as the ordinary posture of a Christian life, not a special measure for crisis moments.

## Grace that finishes the work

The letter's closing prayer names God as the one who will "himself perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle" his readers after they've suffered a while (5:10). It's a fitting final note for a letter addressed to exiles under fire — the suffering is real and not denied, but it isn't the last word; restoration is promised as God's own finishing work, not something the readers have to manufacture themselves.

> *"Be sober and self-controlled. Be watchful. Your adversary the devil walks around like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour."* — 1 Peter 5:8

## Questions for study

1. What would it look like for the leaders you know to shepherd "willingly" and "eagerly" rather than under obligation (5:2)?
2. Where in your life do you need to be more watchful, per verse 8, rather than assuming vigilance is only for a crisis?
3. How does the promise in 5:10 — that God himself perfects and establishes you after suffering — change how you're facing your current hardship?
