Peter goes back to fishing — a strange move after everything that's happened, but a believable one. Grief and uncertainty send people back to what they know how to do with their hands. He fishes all night and catches nothing, until a stranger on the shore tells them where to cast and the net comes up bursting (21:6). John recognizes him first — *"It is the Lord!"* — and Peter, characteristically, jumps in the water rather than wait for the boat.

## Charcoal fire, again

Jesus has breakfast waiting over a charcoal fire (21:9) — the same detail John used to set the scene of Peter's three denials by another charcoal fire (18:18). The echo isn't accidental. This meal is set up to undo what that courtyard did.

## Three questions

Three times Jesus asks Peter some version of "Do you love me?" — matching, round for round, Peter's three denials. By the third round Peter is grieved, but he answers the same way each time: *"Lord, you know everything. You know that I have affection for you"* (21:17). Jesus doesn't demand penance. He gives Peter a job: *"Feed my sheep."*

## More than can be written

The Gospel closes with a line that gestures past its own pages: *"there are also many other things which Jesus did, which if they would all be written... I suppose that even the world itself wouldn't have room for the books"* (21:25). A deliberately overflowing ending for a story that isn't really over.

## Questions for study

1. Why might Jesus restore Peter at a charcoal fire specifically, rather than somewhere neutral?
2. Three questions for three denials — what does that pattern tell you about how restoration works?
3. Jesus answers Peter's failure with a job, not a lecture. What does that suggest about how he handles yours?
4. What does the deliberately "unfinished" ending of verse 25 suggest about your own place in the story?
