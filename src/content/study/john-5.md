The man at the pool of Bethesda has been sick for thirty-eight years, and when Jesus asks if he wants to get well, his answer isn't yes, it's an explanation of why he keeps failing to get into the water in time. Thirty-eight years of almost. Jesus doesn't work with the water at all. He just tells the man to get up.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible john 5`

## An instant where nothing had worked
No ritual, no waiting for the water to stir, just a command.

> *"Jesus said to him, 'Arise, take up your mat, and walk.'"* — John 5:8

## Working on the Sabbath
The healing itself doesn't cause the uproar, doing it on the Sabbath does, and Jesus makes it worse, from his accusers' view, by explaining that his Father is still working, so he is too. That claim, putting himself on equal footing with God's own Sabbath-keeping, escalates the conflict from a rule violation to a much larger dispute about identity.

## Scriptures that point past themselves
Jesus tells the religious leaders they search the Scriptures diligently and still miss what those Scriptures are actually about.

> *"You search the Scriptures... and these are they which testify about me."* — John 5:39

Knowing the text isn't the same as recognizing what, or who, it points to.

## Questions for study
1. What does the man's answer to "do you want to be healed" reveal about thirty-eight years of disappointment?
2. Why does Jesus's explanation about his Father working make the conflict worse, not better?
3. What's the difference between searching Scripture and recognizing what it points to?
4. Where in your life have you settled into "almost" the way the man at the pool had?
