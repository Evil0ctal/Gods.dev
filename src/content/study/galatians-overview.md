Paul opens every one of his other letters with some form of thanks for the reader. Galatians skips that entirely and goes straight to a rebuke. That missing paragraph tells you what kind of letter this is — not a check-in, a rescue mission.

> Read it in the terminal: `bible galatians 1`

## What went wrong at Galatia

Gentile believers were being told that faith in Christ was a fine start but incomplete — that real belonging required circumcision and law-keeping on top of it. Chapters 1-2 defend where Paul's gospel came from (a direct revelation, not a committee decision) and recount him publicly confronting Peter at Antioch for withdrawing from Gentile believers under pressure. For Paul the issue isn't etiquette; it's what the gospel actually is.

## The argument: promise came before law

Chapters 3-4 make the case historically. Abraham was counted righteous by faith centuries before the law existed; the law functioned as a guardian for a season, not a permanent gate. Add law-keeping as a requirement for belonging, Paul argues, and you have quietly redefined what saves a person.

## Freedom with teeth

Chapter 5 turns freedom into an ethic rather than a slogan:

> *"Stand firm therefore in the liberty by which Christ has made us free, and don't be entangled again with a yoke of bondage."* — Galatians 5:1

The same freedom that refuses law-keeping also refuses selfishness — the fruit of the Spirit (5:22-23) is Paul's real alternative to both legalism and license.

## Questions for study

1. Why might "faith plus a little law" be more dangerous, in Paul's view, than open unbelief?
2. Where do you quietly add unwritten requirements to what it means to belong somewhere?
3. Read Galatians 5:22-23 as a description of freedom rather than a checklist. What would that look like in your week?
